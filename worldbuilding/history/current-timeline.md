# History and the present crisis

Status: relative draft chronology with full-story spoilers. No absolute calendar is settled. Chapters identify sources, not a limit on the epic. Latest author decisions supersede earlier sketches; recovered concepts remain separately labeled.

## The inherited highland history

**Established as the prologue's account:** [Bakhtar](../places/bakhtar.md) remembers incursions by sea-kings, horse-lords, and the Lysandrian phalanx. Iskandar campaigns for three years, marries Rukhsana, and remains after his army refuses to march east. His empire fragments within a year of his leaving it. Its provinces and successors remain unspecified.

**Contested within the world:** Iskandar dies of fever or a knife; neither version is established as objective truth. The unnamed cave and the saying about the mountain belong to remembered tradition. Additional eyewitness accounts remain unwritten.

**Current narrative repair:** Fifty thousand is explicitly a refrain, not a survivor count. Households, terraces, grain stores, and repaired watercourses replace earlier universal claims about timeless warfare and citylessness.

Source: [The Conqueror](../../src/content/chapters/00-the-conqueror.ts). The author now places Amara's origin in [Lysandria](../places/lysandria.md), an island with two massive mountains. Her older Bakhtari ancestry is a separate unresolved relationship, not evidence she was born in Bakhtar or knows the genealogy.

## The order already governing Carthara

**Established in current draft:** Before Hanno becomes publicly influential, the Three Seats govern Carthara. Numarius controls the fields, Corvo the harbor, and Vael the garrison. The guard enforces debts as well as public order. The Seats profit from Ravash through the Silken Chain; supporting author notes identify Corvo as its concealed leader. That knowledge is not automatically available to Dyia or the public.

**Interpretation:** Food, shipping, and force bind the offices together. Foundation, succession, and any greater sovereign remain unwritten. No source makes them successors of Iskandar's empire.

Sources: [Three Seats](../factions/the-three-seats.md), [City Guard](../factions/the-city-guard.md), [Silken Chain](../factions/the-silken-chain.md).

## Leonin welcome, dispossession, and diaspora

**Adopted author history:** Numarius's people lost their homeland and arrived as refugees. Leonin communities welcomed them. Their guests' people later consolidated power and dispossessed their former hosts. Lord Numarius now heads the hostile order. Whether the first arrival falls within his lifetime or an earlier generation remains unresolved; do not invent a lifespan to settle it.

The Leonin are lion-people driven into refugee camps in the Tengeri Wastes and dispersed throughout the world. Many are enslaved; repeated bombardment, forced labor, and restricted movement continue during Hanno's visit. Every Leonin carries the lost home's flag. Peace advocates, fighters willing to resist to the death, camp councils, and diaspora networks disagree about their future.

[Chapter Three](../../src/content/chapters/03-journeys.ts) shows Chuluun's own coerced labor, inhabited facilities attacked, and false official claims of absent civilians and voluntary transfers. Wider powers repeat the propaganda. An acknowledged convoy attack and an unverified allegation remain distinct from those demonstrated lies. The [Leonin dossier](../factions/the-leonin.md) links the four working factions.

## Recovered histories awaiting placement

**Early-source concepts:** The [geography document](../sources/early-geography.md) gives a fifteen-tribe Khanate, a missing tribe, an empty Golden Yurt at Erdene Zuu, and Chuluun as an Altan prince. Their relationship to displacement remains open. The source's freely inhabited ancestral-Tengeri premise is superseded; its snapshot remains intact.

The same source gives Ruhania two regions around the Jahnavi, an abandoned observatory at Zulfaar, a buried city at Al-Qamar Al-Khafiq, and a submerged palace of a lost dynasty at Jannat Al-Rutbah. Ruins supply historical questions; stories of curses and haunting supply beliefs. They do not establish supernatural causes. Ruhania's position relative to Carthara remains unplaced.
