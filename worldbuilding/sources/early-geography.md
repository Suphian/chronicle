**Ruhania**

Ruhania, the homeland of the Ruhaniyya people, is a realm of diverse landscapes, marked by the mighty **river Jahnavi,** This river not only feeds the land but also serves as a geographical divider, creating two distinct regions with their unique capitals, peoples, languages, and cultures.

The eastern region of Ruhania is a sprawling desert known as the **Qa'hir**, a vast expanse of sun-baked dunes and rugged outcrops. Scattered throughout the desert are life-giving oases, lush with date palms, olive trees, and other forms of plant life. The heart of this region is the capital city of **Qalb Al-Sahra,** a vibrant oasis metropolis, renowned as a center of trade and intellectual pursuits. The desert-dwelling Ruhaniyya are recognized for their resilience and adaptability, co-existing harmoniously with the harsh landscape. They are skilled in desert survival, and their deep knowledge of local flora and fauna is well respected.

To the western of the **Jahnavi River**, the land transforms into lush wetlands, a vibrant contrast to the desert's arid expanse. This region is home to **Al-Rutbah**, the second capital, a city built on stilts and interconnected with a labyrinth of waterways. People in this area have developed a culture that's deeply intertwined with the water and the fertile land, becoming skilled farmers and fishers. Their language, though bearing similarities to their desert counterparts, has distinct linguistic features influenced by their unique lifestyle.

Towering mountain ranges flank Ruhania to the north, serving as natural barriers and rich sources of minerals. These mountains contribute to the kingdom's wealth and serve as strategic defense points.

To the west, the tranquil **Azure Sea** extends its arm, its waters teeming with marine life. Coastal cities line the shore, bustling with activity as goods from across Ruhania and beyond are bartered and sold. The Ruhaniyya are skilled seafarers, harnessing the sea's bounty for trade and exploration.

Despite the varied and challenging conditions of their homeland, the Ruhaniyya people have not only survived but thrived, turning Ruhania into a prosperous and vibrant kingdom that reflects the diversity and adaptability of its people.

The northern mountain range is known as the **"Safar Peaks,"** a name that speaks to the adventurous journey or "safar" in Arabic that these mountains represent.

There is a significant pass in the Safar Peaks, an important route for trade and travel. This passage, known for its high altitude and breathtaking vistas, is called the "**Zephyr Pass.**" The name Zephyr, derived from the ancient Greek god of the west wind, is a fitting name for a place known for its cool, refreshing breezes that provide respite to travelers crossing the rugged terrain.

**The Sanctuary of Celestial Harmony**

Location:  
The main temple of the Solunari religion, the Sanctuary of Celestial Harmony, is located on a large island to the south, known as "Astral Isle." This island is renowned for its pristine beaches, lush forests, and crystal-clear waters. It is believed that the unique positioning of the island, combined with its natural beauty, makes it a powerful conduit for the energies of Solara and Lunaris.

Temple Description:  
The Sanctuary of Celestial Harmony is an architectural marvel, designed to honor both Solara and Lunaris and to symbolize the balance they bring to the world. The temple is divided into two main sections, one dedicated to each deity, connected by a central courtyard.

The Sun Temple, dedicated to Solara, is built of golden sandstone and features intricate carvings depicting scenes of life, growth, and prosperity. Large windows and skylights allow sunlight to flood the interior, creating a warm and inviting atmosphere. At the heart of the Sun Temple lies a magnificent statue of Solara, adorned with precious gems and surrounded by offerings from her devotees.

The Moon Temple, dedicated to Lunaris, is constructed of silver-white marble, with delicate engravings that depict the phases of the moon and the cycle of death and rebirth. The temple's interior is dimly lit by soft, ethereal light, creating an air of mystery and tranquility. At the center of the Moon Temple stands an elegant statue of Lunaris, his form bathed in the gentle glow of moonlight, with offerings of night-blooming flowers and small tokens placed at his feet.

The central courtyard of the Sanctuary of Celestial Harmony is a lush garden, filled with plants and trees that represent the union of Solara and Lunaris. A serene pool of water lies at the heart of the courtyard, reflecting the light of both the sun and the moon, symbolizing their eternal dance of balance and harmony.

Ceremonies and Events:  
The Sanctuary of Celestial Harmony serves as the spiritual center for the Solunari faith and hosts numerous ceremonies and events throughout the year. Key events include:

1\. The Solstice and Equinox Festivals: These grand celebrations, held during the summer and winter solstices and the spring and autumn equinoxes, attract worshippers from far and wide, who gather at the Sanctuary to pay homage to Solara and Lunaris and to participate in the festivities.

2\. Initiation Ceremonies: New priests and priestesses of the Solunari faith are initiated at the Sanctuary, undergoing sacred rites and rituals that deepen their connection to Solara and Lunaris and prepare them for their roles as spiritual leaders.

3\. Pilgrimages: Devotees of the Solunari faith journey from across the land to visit the Sanctuary of Celestial Harmony, seeking the blessings and guidance of their deities and participating in rituals and prayers.

4\. Moonlit Vigils: The Sanctuary serves as the primary site for the monthly Moonlit Vigils, during which followers of Lunaris gather to meditate, share their dreams, and seek the god's wisdom and guidance.

The Sanctuary of Celestial Harmony is not only a place of worship but also a symbol of unity and balance, embodying the core beliefs of the Solunari religion and serving as a beacon of hope and inspiration for its followers.

**1\. Ruins of Al-Qamar Al-Khafiq:**  
Located deep within the Qa'hir Desert, the Ruins of Al-Qamar Al-Khafiq, or "The Sinking Moon," are the remnants of an ancient Ruhaniyya city swallowed by the sands over centuries. This once-thriving metropolis was believed to have been cursed by the moon deity for its inhabitants' hubris and greed. The ruins are now home to mysterious and dangerous creatures, and treasure hunters who venture into the shifting sands seeking the city's legendary riches rarely return. The Ruhaniyya people see the Ruins of Al-Qamar Al-Khafiq as a cautionary tale, reminding them of the consequences of excessive pride and arrogance.

**2\. The Sunken Gardens of Jannat Al-Rutbah:**  
In the western region, near the Zulimar Wetlands, lie the Sunken Gardens of Jannat Al-Rutbah. Once a magnificent garden and palace complex built by a long-lost Ruhaniyya dynasty, the Sunken Gardens now lie partially submerged beneath the wetlands' waters. The palace's intricate architecture and the remnants of its once-lush gardens are still visible, but the encroaching waters and thick vegetation have rendered it nearly inaccessible. The Ruhaniyya people believe the Sunken Gardens to be haunted by the spirits of the royal family who once lived there, and they are considered a place of sorrow and loss.

**3\. The Tower of Zulfaar:**  
Situated in the Safar Peaks near the Zephyr Pass, the Tower of Zulfaar was once an observatory and temple dedicated to the worship of the sun and moon deities. Over time, the temple fell into disrepair and was abandoned, leaving only the enigmatic tower standing as a testament to its former glory. The Ruhaniyya people consider the Tower of Zulfaar a sacred site, and many pilgrims undertake a journey to its remote location seeking spiritual enlightenment and connection with the celestial powers.

**Sidrat Al Muntaha**

The Kingdom of Sidrat Al Muntaha is a vibrant tapestry of cultures, landscapes, and natural wonders, woven together by the threads of its inhabitants' shared respect for the environment.

Rugged mountain ranges, known as the **Veins of Sidrat,** divide the kingdom into distinct regions, each with its own identity. These mountains are peppered with structures of marvel \- a network of bridges, each a feat of engineering prowess. The bridges, often carved out of the mountains themselves, not only serve as physical connectors but also symbolize the unity of the kingdom's diverse cultures. They ascend into the clouds, their stone arches covered in moss and lichen, blending seamlessly into the natural landscape.

The kingdom's connectivity is further enhanced by an intricate network of roads, known as the **Sidrat Web**. These roads are unique for they are not merely paved paths. Instead, they are lined with bioluminescent plants that glow softly at night, guiding travellers while reducing the need for artificial lighting. This environmentally friendly approach to infrastructure reflects the kingdom's core principle of living in harmony with nature.

Off these main roads, the land gives way to more remote, rural areas, each with its own unique culture and traditions. These villages are often tucked away in quiet valleys, hidden within dense forests or perched on cliff tops. The locals, known as the Keepers, are known for their unique practices and customs, which have remained untouched by time. Despite their distance from the bustling city-centers, they are never completely isolated, thanks to the Sidrat Web.

The kingdom is also home to an assortment of unique flora, some found nowhere else in the world. **The Glowblossom Trees, the Lumine Vines, and the Whispering Reeds** are just a few examples of the kingdom's unique botanical treasures. The citizens of Sidrat Al Muntaha, regardless of their region, share a deep reverence for these natural marvels, viewing them as divine gifts.

The kingdom is a sanctuary, ensuring that all who traverse its lands can do so freely, without fear of attack by people or wildlife. Provisions are abundant, and travelers can count on the generosity of locals and the fertility of the land to sustain them. This sense of security pervades all aspects of life, contributing to the kingdom's serene atmosphere.

Sidrat Al Muntaha is a kingdom of contrasts, where the rush of waterfalls can be heard alongside the whisper of the wind through the mountain passes, where the ancient culture lives in harmony with progressive environmental practices. This is a land where the journey is as breathtaking as the destination, a place where every stone, tree, and river has a story to tell.

**Lakes**  
1\. Lake Al-Fajr (The Dawn): This is the largest of the seven lakes, named for the way the first light of dawn reflects off its calm waters, creating an awe-inspiring sight. According to legend, it was here that the first people of Sidrat Al Muntaha received divine guidance from their deity, leading to the establishment of the kingdom.

2\. Lake Siraj (The Lantern): The second-largest lake, Al-Siraj is home to a rare species of bioluminescent algae that causes the water to glow softly at night, reminiscent of a lantern's light. The local people believe that this phenomenon is a symbol of the deity's presence and protection over the kingdom.

3\. Lake Hikma (The Wisdom): The third-largest lake, Al-Hikma, is surrounded by a dense forest filled with ancient trees. These trees are said to have been planted by the first scholars and sages of Sidrat Al Muntaha as a symbol of their devotion to the pursuit of knowledge and wisdom. The lake itself is believed to possess healing properties, and many travel great distances to seek its waters.

4\. Lake Salam (The Peace): This small, serene lake is known for its crystal-clear waters, which remain undisturbed even in the strongest winds. It is a popular destination for meditation and spiritual retreats, as its tranquil atmosphere is believed to promote inner peace and reflection.

5\. Lake Amal (The Hope): Located in a valley surrounded by steep cliffs, Lake Al-Amal is a hidden gem accessible only by a narrow mountain pass. The journey to reach the lake is treacherous, but those who persevere are rewarded with the sight of its pristine waters, which are said to represent the hope that endures even in the darkest of times.

6\. Lake Wadood (The Loving): This heart-shaped lake is a popular destination for couples and families, as it is believed that the waters of Wadood possess the power to strengthen the bonds of love and friendship. A yearly festival is held on its shores, during which people exchange tokens of affection and renew their vows of love and loyalty.

7\. Lake Baraka (The Blessing): The smallest of the seven lakes, Baraka, is nestled within a lush, fertile valley. The waters of this lake are said to have been blessed by the deity of Sidrat Al Muntaha, imbuing them with the power to bring prosperity and abundance to the surrounding lands. Farmers and merchants from all over the kingdom come to the lake to seek its blessings for a successful harvest and thriving business.

**Mountains**   
1\. Jabal Al-Nur (Mountain of Light): The tallest and most revered of the three mountains, Jabal Al-Nur is said to be the birthplace of the kingdom's religion. It is believed that the deity of Sidrat Al Muntaha first revealed their divine teachings to the people at the peak of this mountain. A sacred temple has been built at the summit, where devout pilgrims journey to pay their respects and seek guidance.

2\. Jabal Al-Sirr (Mountain of Secrets): This enigmatic mountain is known for its hidden caves and labyrinthine passages that weave throughout its interior. Ancient scrolls and texts suggest that Jabal Al-Sirr was once a place of great learning and wisdom, where scholars, philosophers, and mystics gathered to unravel the mysteries of the universe. Although many of its secrets have been lost to time, some believe that remnants of this ancient knowledge still lie hidden within the mountain's depths, waiting to be discovered by those brave enough to venture into its shadows.

3\. Jabal Al-Hifaz (Mountain of Guardians): Jabal Al-Hifaz is a formidable mountain that stands as a natural barrier between Sidrat Al Muntaha and the hostile kingdom to the west. It is said that the spirits of the kingdom's greatest warriors reside on this mountain, eternally protecting the land they once called home. The mountain is also home to a highly defended fortress, which serves as both a training ground for the military forces of Sidrat Al Muntaha and a vital defense against invaders from the west.

**Erdene Zuu The Capital of Tengeri Wastes**

Situated in the heart of a vast mountainous wasteland, the Tengeri Wastes serve as the ancestral homeland for the Leonin tribes. This harsh, unforgiving landscape has molded the Leonin into the resilient and fierce beings they are today. The terrain is treacherous, characterized by jagged peaks, sprawling canyons, arid riverbeds, and extensive bone graveyards \- a solemn reminder of the struggles faced by the tribes over generations.

The Tengeri Wastes are punctuated by sporadic patches of barren vegetation, the only signs of life in the otherwise desolate expanse. These patches, though scarce, are treasured by the Leonin as they provide the only source of food and materials for their simple nomadic dwellings.

Amidst the arid landscape, the Altan and Tsetseg Tribes, among others, have carved out a life in this unforgiving wilderness. They've built robust communities that thrive despite the scarcity of resources. The tribes have learned to harness the power of the winds that whip through the canyons, using them to their advantage in hunting and warfare. Over time, they've developed a deep spiritual connection to these winds, considering them to be whispers of their ancestors guiding them through life.

Capital: Erdene Zuu

In the heart of the Tengeri Wastes, nestled within the deepest canyon, lies Erdene Zuu, the capital of the Leonin tribes. It's not a grand city with towering structures, but rather a sprawling shantytown, a testament to the nomadic nature of its inhabitants.

Erdene Zuu, meaning "Hundred Treasures", isn't named for material wealth but rather the richness of culture, tradition, and community spirit that thrives within its boundaries. The buildings are an eclectic mix of makeshift dwellings, constructed from the bones of fallen beasts and the sparse vegetation of the wasteland. The town's layout is ever-changing, reflecting the nomadic spirit of its inhabitants who rearrange their homes according to the seasons, the wind, and the movements of the wildlife.

Despite the harsh conditions, Erdene Zuu is vibrant and alive. The air is filled with the sound of traditional music, the scent of communal meals being prepared, and the sight of young Leonin sparring with each other, honing their combat skills. The town square is marked by a towering monolith, a relic from a forgotten age, and serves as a gathering place for the tribes during times of celebration, mourning, or decision-making.

Erdene Zuu is a symbol of resilience, a testament to the Leonin's ability to thrive in the face of adversity. It's a place where the old ways are respected, and the tribe's history is passed down from generation to generation. For Chuluun, Erdene Zuu is more than just a capital; it's a beacon calling him back to his roots, to the responsibilities he once shied away from, and to a destiny that might yet be fulfilled.

**The Khanate of the Leonin Tribes \- The Fragmented Khanate**

Once a unified political entity, the Khanate of the Leonin Tribes consisted of fifteen independent tribes, each contributing to the strength and resilience of the collective Leonin society. Each tribe occupied a distinct region of the expansive and harsh Tengeri Wastes, adapting and evolving its practices to thrive in the specific conditions of its territory.

These tribes, including notable ones like the Altan and Tsetseg, operated autonomously, honoring their unique customs, traditions, and self-governance. However, all were bound by the authority of the Khan, a figurehead who ensured unity, arbitrated intertribal disputes, and led the collective defense against external threats.

However, for many generations now, no Khan has presided over the tribes. The Khanate has descended into dormancy, its unity fractured. A series of unfortunate events, including natural disasters, internal conflict, and foreign invasions, contributed to the fall of the Khanate, but one event is recalled with particular pain \- the loss of the fifteenth tribe.

The fifteenth tribe, whose name is spoken in hushed whispers, disappeared mysteriously, leaving behind only abandoned dwellings and unanswered questions. Their loss shook the Leonin society, driving the final wedge in the already crumbling unity of the Khanate.

Today, the remaining fourteen tribes continue their existence in the Tengeri Wastes, holding onto their customs and traditions, but the unity and strength once offered by the Khanate are distant memories. Intertribal disputes have become more frequent, and the collective defense is weakened in the absence of a unifying Khan.

The Golden Yurt, once the symbol of the Khan's authority and the Khanate's unity, stands empty in the heart of Erdene Zuu. The seat of a power that once was, it now serves as a poignant reminder of the unity that once prevailed among the Leonin tribes. It stands as a silent testament to the strength and resilience of the Leonin people, and a somber monument to their lost brethren.

Without the Khanate, the tribes have become more insular, focusing on their survival in the unforgiving Tengeri Wastes. Though they share common blood and history, the absence of the fifteenth tribe and the vacant position of the Khan have created visible rifts among them. Disputes over resources, territorial boundaries, and old rivalries have intensified, threatening the delicate balance that keeps the tribes from spiraling into chaos.

Yet, even in these trying times, there's a shared longing among the tribes for the return of the Khanate. Many Leonin elders recount tales of the once flourishing Khanate to their young ones, embedding in them the hope for a leader who can reunite the tribes and restore their former glory. The tribes continue to uphold their traditions, train their warriors, and hold tribal councils, keeping the spirit of the Khanate alive.

Amidst this, the dormant Khanate awaits the rise of a new Khan, a leader who can mend the fractured bonds and reunite the tribes. The lost tribe continues to be a wound in the heart of the Leonin people, a mystery they yearn to solve, and a reminder of a unity they desire to reclaim.

In this scenario, Chuluun, the prince of the Altan Tribe, finds himself on a journey of self-discovery and responsibility. His choices could significantly impact the future of the Leonin tribes, potentially even heralding the revival of the dormant Khanate.
