# Chapter One: scene plans

Status: editorial plan for the first prose pass of [Beneath the Market Awnings](../../src/content/chapters/01-market-awnings.ts). Existing plot events are **draft continuity**; the goals, opposition, and staging below include **proposed development**. Check the actual chapter for what the prose has established. These notes are not an independent manuscript or a claim that every suggestion has been implemented.

The chapter retains the slug `market-awnings`, its `title` card, and all eight existing narrative scene IDs. A scene heading marks a navigable passage; it need not mean a reset to a synopsis. Future expansion targets live in the [opening arc](opening-arc.md).

## The Fruit Stall — `the-fruit-stall`

**Viewpoint:** Hanno. **Place:** Carthara market. **Immediate goal:** help Adil sell enough fruit to make the day's work worthwhile. **Opposition:** perishable stock, bargaining customers, stronger vendors, and obligations that consume the margin. **Turn:** a modest sale makes the difference between what the boy sees as success and what his father can afford. **Consequence:** Hanno begins to associate usefulness with relief from his father's anxiety.

**Introduce:** Adil as a merchant with technique, pride, humor, and ordinary imperfections; Hanno's attentive but incomplete reading of adults; the lived market. **Transition:** carry earnings, unsold fruit, or an unfinished household errand upstairs. Avoid an omniscient announcement that all bystanders will someday deserve Hanno's revenge.

## Above the Pottery Shop — `above-the-pottery-shop`

**Viewpoint:** Hanno, or a clearly separated Amara viewpoint if the prose uses one. **Place:** the family's original rooms and pottery workspace. **Immediate goal:** Amara must complete usable work while the family manages food and earnings. **Opposition:** time, material costs, children needing attention, and disagreement about money. **Turn:** an ordinary domestic compromise briefly sustains them without solving the precarious finances. **Consequence:** the later wheel seizure removes an established means of making a life, not an ornamental symbol introduced solely for tragedy.

**Introduce:** Dyia through action; Amara's professional judgment and old tune; the warmth and friction of an actual household. **Transition:** mark the passage into the dry season through prices, water, or work. Do not convert the tune into proof of a known royal lineage.

## The Drought — `the-drought`

**Viewpoint:** Hanno. **Place:** market and the approaches used for deliveries. **Immediate goal:** help Adil keep the stall trading through a failed season. **Opposition:** scarce fruit, rising purchase costs, fixed debt demands, and a creditor unwilling to extend time. **Turn:** Numarius's agents arrive to seize stock and the stall; Adil's promise of another season has no bargaining force. **Consequence:** the family loses income precisely when repayment is demanded.

**Introduce:** the visible distinction between an amount owed and a means of paying it; administrative force operating through familiar commercial space. **Transition:** follow the agents' action directly into Adil's removal. Avoid making a future legal explanation sound like a settled constitution absent from the sources.

## Beyond Mercy — `the-fields`

**Viewpoint:** Hanno. **Physical place:** the market during the taking; the fields east of Carthara are the destination being threatened or described. **Immediate goal:** prevent Adil's removal, or make someone with power respond to him. **Opposition:** armed agents, the crowd's fears and obligations, and the child's limited leverage. **Turn:** the appeal fails and Adil is taken. **Consequence:** Hanno remembers a public refusal to intervene; his later generalized blame is his interpretation, not an objective verdict on every vendor.

**Introduce:** debt enforcement's human cost; witnesses who have reasons for silence, however inadequate. **Transition:** return to the household's immediate need for earnings. Preserve the scene ID `the-fields`, while ensuring a map link distinguishes the destination from Hanno's physical position.

## Master Sinna — `the-apothecary`

**Viewpoint:** Hanno, with Amara's bargaining visible through dialogue. **Place:** Sinna's Cartharan shop. **Immediate goal:** secure work and learn something useful enough to support the family. **Opposition:** an unequal bargain, basic tasks, mistakes, and a master who controls access to training and customers. **Turn:** Hanno sees that careful work can help someone and is drawn toward its possibilities. **Consequence:** time and labor become attached to Sinna's terms; developing skill does not automatically create independence.

**Introduce:** a competent teacher capable of exploitation; alchemy as learned attention, not inherited mastery. **Transition:** explicitly mark months or seasons of work before later family deterioration and guard recruitment. An observed improvement in a small task should precede claims that Hanno produces unprecedented cures.

## Ravash — `ravash`

**Viewpoint:** Dyia, clearly signaled. **Place:** home and Carthara's public streets. **Immediate goal:** understand what is happening to Amara and obtain enough security to change it. **Opposition:** limited income, Hanno's absence, Amara's privacy and distress, and the offers attached to paid authority. **Turn:** Dyia accepts a guard post because he believes influence inside it can protect the family. **Consequence:** his intended solution makes him answerable to people who enforce the debt.

**Introduce:** observation rather than expert diagnosis; Amara's life beyond symptoms; a credible path from recruitment to service. **Transition:** the uniform becomes an obligation when the eviction order arrives. Do not repeat the later Chapter Two discovery of the specific seller before it occurs, or invent Sinna as the original supplier.

## The Door — `the-door`

**Viewpoint:** Dyia. **Place:** the original rooms above the pottery shop. **Immediate goal:** keep the post while finding a way to spare his mother. **Opposition:** his sergeant demands compliance and the order reaches his own household. **Turn:** Dyia steps aside. **Consequence:** Amara loses the rooms and wheel; Dyia keeps his position and a rationalization he will later call protection.

**Introduce:** Amara's reaction and the practical work of leaving; where she can sleep next and what portable possessions survive. **Transition:** establish the temporary living arrangement, then the later formal proceeding against Adil. Hanno must remain unaware that Dyia participated in the eviction until Amara tells him in `something-breaking`.

## The Sentencing — `the-sentencing`

**Viewpoint:** Hanno. **Place:** a Cartharan public hearing. **Immediate goal:** get a hearing for the family's need or learn a definite way to bring Adil home. **Opposition:** the authority formalizing the debt and Dyia's visible obedience beside it. **Turn:** Adil is formally condemned; Hanno's brother neither speaks nor steps forward. **Consequence:** Hanno reads the uniform as a choice against their father, while Dyia carries a further failure he cannot repair by remaining silent.

**Introduce:** seizure and formal sentencing as distinct events with visible elapsed time; the difference between a crowd and usable help. **Transition:** close on a concrete act, object, or failed exchange that carries into the apprenticeship, rather than explaining the whole future tragedy. Keep Adil's eventual death separate; this is not his death scene.

## Scene-to-scene continuity

Track four changing conditions through this chapter: the household's income and possessions; Hanno's access to work; Dyia's obligation to the guard; and the brothers' unequal knowledge. Dates can remain relative during this pass if transitions are explicit, but the [full age problem](../continuity.md) remains open. A sentence repairing recruitment age or housing does not settle every lifespan or legal mechanism in the world.

The [character profiles](../characters/hanno.md), [original narrative](../sources/early-narrative.md), and [author decisions](../decisions.md) constrain the draft. The older source lacks the eviction detail; preserve that deliberate repository addition. None of these scene plans determines the action after Dyia's final knock years later.
