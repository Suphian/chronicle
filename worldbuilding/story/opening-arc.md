# Opening arc: what a family owes

Status: **proposed future chapter structure**. The author requested a first prose pass through the existing story before revising individual chapters. The current reading order and chapter maturity remain in the [registry](../../src/content/chapters/index.ts). The six units below identify possible later expansions; they are not six newly published chapters and do not replace existing URLs.

## The promise of the opening

Let the reader know this family before asking them to judge its destruction. A father must sell perishable fruit. A mother must finish paid work. Two brothers want to help and gradually choose incompatible ways to do it. Carthara's beauty and coercion share streets, water, ledgers, and labor. The first arc ends when those methods become separate lives, before distant travel can be mistaken for a cure for the household's problems.

Hanno's chronological family opening is the chosen entry for this prose pass, with the conqueror legend available as optional world reading. The author's affection for *The Count of Monte Cristo* suggests patient development of relationships, obligations, and revenge rather than a lore requirement at the entrance. The investigation opening in [story options](../story-options.md) remains a viable later restructuring, not the current starting point.

## What carries into the next arc

Hanno leaves with a practical commission and unresolved dependence on Sinna. Dyia remains with authority whose conditions he has already accepted. Amara remains a working person with decisions about earnings, care, and contact with her sons. Record what money and assistance actually reach her during each absence; affection does not pay rent by implication.

The next arc can develop the Tengeri Wastes, Sidrat Al Muntaha, and Lysandria through obligations rather than a tour of acquired powers. Chuluun needs interests beyond teaching a visitor. Lysandrian healers should contribute to the sick child's recovery. Alethea must have work and relationships before Hanno becomes her partner. Her decision to leave should change something in her own life.

## Information released and information withheld

- Readers may see Dyia at the eviction; Hanno learns of it only at the later family confrontation. Avoid narration that quietly gives him the reader's knowledge.
- Early Ravash scenes establish Amara's experience and Dyia's observations, not the full Silken Chain hierarchy or the Seats' concealed profit.
- The workshop can reveal that a remedy is sold under Sinna's name before Hanno recognizes the full commercial arrangement. His ignorance needs restricted access to clients, records, or terms, not an absence of intelligence.
- Numarius's debt power is visible early. His later strategic reasoning and relationship to Corvo and Vael are not knowledge the child Hanno automatically possesses.
- Amara's songs can remain remembered practices. They do not establish a divine mission or an ancestral revelation for her sons.
