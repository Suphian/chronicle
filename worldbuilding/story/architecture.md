# Story architecture

Status: **working architecture**, grounded in current draft continuity, with proposals labeled below. The author approved a readable story, connected world reference, and visible writing room, and requested a first prose pass across the existing story before later chapter revisions. The first pass starts with Hanno and the family in Carthara; the conqueror legend is optional world reading. This document does not settle the epic's final scope or Dyia's action at the door. Published chapter maturity comes from the [chapter registry](../../src/content/chapters/index.ts), not a manually maintained count here.

## The dramatic question

**Can the people who learned to survive Carthara's coercion protect one another without making that coercion their own?**

This candidate question connects the existing family tragedy with a larger civic story. Hanno seeks the freedom and recognition his father was denied. Dyia seeks the authority to prevent another dispossession. Alethea seeks to practice care in a city that converts useful work into someone else's property. Each can do real good; each needs other people whose interests cannot be reduced to that good.

The first two aims are interpretations of [Hanno](../characters/hanno.md) and [Dyia's](../characters/dyia.md) current actions. Alethea's independent practice, treatment boundaries, and financial decisions now have scenes in the prose draft; her institution can continue to develop beyond them. The larger political outcome remains open. See [three possible primary stories](../story-options.md) for different structural possibilities.

The author names *The Count of Monte Cristo* as a favorite. Use that taste to guide patient development, consequential conversations, intersecting obligations, the accumulation of power, and revenge that demands preparation and exacts moral costs. Do not copy Dantès's plot or manufacture archaic prose. Begin with Hanno trying to do something comprehensible before requiring the reader to study history.

**Hanno's specific character reference is Walter White in *Breaking Bad*.** This is adopted author direction: technical talent, calculation, pride, self-justification, and a capacity for deliberate killing. He is a decent fighter rather than an exceptional combatant. His menace should come from what he knows, what he arranges, and how he uses other people's trust. Give those plans costs and failure points; do not rescue him with unexplained fighting mastery or omniscience.

**Zaharaz is an ordinary wooden staff.** Keep the name and the stable source scene ID `the-spear`, but remove the superseded double-bladed weapon and false palindrome claims from active descriptions. Phylios's gift marks gratitude for medical work and is useful on a road. Its emotional significance does not confer extraordinary capabilities. Five years on Lysandria are chiefly years of alchemical study and work, with occasional basic self-defense practice.

Use one close viewpoint per scene. Hanno, Dyia, Amara, and Alethea can carry sustained scenes; the three rulers need not all become viewpoint characters. A regional viewpoint earns space by pursuing an objective that exists without the Averroes family. Do not add a distant character solely to explain a map pin.

## Causal movement across the present material

1. Drought reduces Idris's margin; debt enforcement takes the stall and his labor. The loss of earnings drives Amara's apprenticeship bargain and Dyia's search for paid authority.
2. Apprenticeship keeps Hanno useful but dependent. Guard service keeps Dyia employed but complicit. Their incompatible accounts of sacrifice break the household's ability to cooperate.
3. Travel brings knowledge, commercial obligations, and relationships. The Leonin camps confront Hanno with displacement, enslavement, and the gap between lived events and an official account. On Lysandria, an island with two enormous mountains and Amara's place of origin, his alchemical work gives him an opportunity to care without owning the recipient. His relationship with Alethea begins there; shared island origin does not establish kinship between her and Amara.
4. Returning to Carthara makes independence urgent. The cure changes who receives money, credit, and public gratitude. Alethea and Amara must have choices within that success.
5. Sinna attacks both a person and confidence in the cure. Hanno's retaliation through the stolen shipment establishes a deliberate use of lethal deception. His belief that he has earned an exception matters more than a claim that grief made him powerless.
6. Public esteem brings Hanno closer to Numarius. The banquet exposes the persistence of debtor coercion beneath prosperity. Manufacturing Dust is Hanno's decision, not the automatic consequence of an insult.
7. Distribution creates harmed people, records, money, and witnesses. Dyia's investigation and Alethea's work constrain concealment. The garden and the final knock are the current draft's convergence; neither grants automatic redemption or resolves the wider city.

These links summarize current events with editorial interpretations. They do not prove that the existing chapters already dramatize every intermediate step. The [continuity audit](../continuity.md) records missing evidence and mechanics.

## Material stakes and the wider epic

Food, water, debt, medicine, shipping, and enforcement should repeatedly cross one another. A seized wheel costs Amara income; a delayed ingredient costs a clinic capacity; a missing hearing record costs Dyia a lawful route. Explain who works, pays, authorizes access, and bears failure before adding another council or war.

The [Leonin](../factions/the-leonin.md) now have adopted displacement and enslavement history: destroyed homeland, Tengeri refugee camps, repeated bombardment, propaganda, worldwide diaspora, and the lost-home flag. Numarius's people were once welcomed as refugees and later dispossessed their hosts. The founding generation's relationship to the present lord remains to be dated. Chuluun belongs within this coercion, while competing Leonin responses create political choices independent of Hanno. The [Surety Quarter](../ideas/the-surety-quarter.md) proposes a further Cartharan connection; that district and route are not yet chapter events.

The recovered [geography](../sources/early-geography.md) also supplies Ruhania's institutions, the Grand Codexium, and Sidrat's sanctuary. Their continental placement, route times, and the relationship of older Leonin clan history to present displacement remain unresolved in the [geography reconciliation](../geography-reconciliation.md). Let each region make consequential choices before its resources become convenient help for Carthara.

The [pilgrim-ship pitch](../ideas/the-pilgrim-ship.md) stays in research and proposed adaptation. A future harbor, witness, protection, or public-memory thread could carry it. Islamic historical inspiration should also include scholarship, worship, charitable care, trade, and disagreement; the atrocity need not become another secret in Hanno's ancestry.

## Prose and pacing standard

A chapter needs an unfolding situation: desire, resistance, action, conversation, a consequential turn, and a changed situation. Give the reader enough physical and emotional time to inhabit it. Use concrete labor, sensory detail, and an individual observer's attention; avoid paragraphs that announce years of development without letting anyone act.

Length follows the scene's work. Roughly 2,500–5,000 words is a useful future revision range for a substantial chapter, not a minimum to pad toward or a cap. A first prose pass can expose where a legacy entry needs to split. Hanno's learning should chiefly concern his technical craft, with failures and dependence on local expertise; self-defense remains an ordinary practical skill. Culture should appear in routines and disputes. Dialogue should contain different wants, not alternating declarations of the theme. Architectural grandeur should include maintenance, access, shade, water, and the people doing the work.

Retain stable source slugs, scene IDs, and place IDs while expanding. New chapter divisions can be introduced later with an explicit migration plan. The prologue is a told legend with competing accounts, not omniscient proof of inherited destiny. History and family motives are not substitutes for a present-tense scene.

## Choices deliberately left open

- The larger epic's ensemble balance and eventual arrangement across books. Hanno's chronological family opening is the chosen entry for this first pass; the investigation-led alternative remains available as a later structural experiment.
- The complete age table around Hanno's recorded age of 115, especially supporting characters who knew his childhood. Local time transitions cannot resolve the whole lifespan question.
- The precise constitution of the Seats, Carthara's relation to a greater sovereign, and travel scale.
- Alethea's final fate in a later structural revision. The existing draft endpoint remains visible until deliberately revised.
- What Dyia knows and does at the final door, and which public consequences follow. No answer is chosen here.
