# Amara Averroes

ID: `amara` · Draft endpoint: Chapter Ten · Dead

## Established in current draft

A Bakhtari potter, wife of [Adil](adil.md), mother of [Hanno](hanno.md) and [Dyia](dyia.md), descended through [Rukhsana](rukhsana.md) and [Iskandar](iskandar.md). She hums cave songs but does not explain their origin to her sons. She arranges Hanno's apprenticeship. Dyia steps aside when the household and her wheel are seized. She uses Ravash, undergoes Hanno's experimental treatment, and recovers. [Virello](virello.md) later deceives her into taking potent Ravash disguised as a stronger remedy, killing her.

Sources: Chapters [Zero](../../src/content/chapters/00-the-conqueror.ts), [One](../../src/content/chapters/01-market-awnings.ts), [Two](../../src/content/chapters/02-bitter-apprenticeship.ts), [Five](../../src/content/chapters/05-the-cure.ts), [Six](../../src/content/chapters/06-shadows-and-accusations.ts).

## Interpretation

Her work and arrangement of the apprenticeship show practical initiative under pressure. Her quiet disclosure that Dyia was at the door breaks the brothers' preferred accounts of themselves. Her silence about Bakhtar is significant, but the reason is not established; it need not hide a royal destiny. Addiction is part of her experience, not the explanation of her entire character.

## Proposed development

Give her a professional standard, customers, and another potter with whom she exchanges help or competes. After the cure, let her want something her sons cannot agree to provide: independent earnings, privacy, or a return journey. Give her words about the risks and burden of being Hanno's first patient. If her ancestry matters, connect it to an actual obligation or remembered practice before connecting it to power.

## Open questions and continuity

Why did she leave Bakhtar, and does she know the genealogy? Where does she live after eviction? What survives of her craft after the wheel is removed? The early sheet leaves her alive after recovery and says Virello originally supplied Ravash to bind Hanno; that is not established by the revised chapters. Her later murder must not erase the recovery or imply she caused her own death by failing morally.
