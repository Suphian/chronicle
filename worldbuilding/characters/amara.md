# Amara Averroes

ID: `amara` · Draft endpoint: Chapter Ten · Dead

## Established in current draft

A potter from [Lysandria](../places/lysandria.md), the island with two enormous mountains, wife of [Adil](adil.md), and mother of [Hanno](hanno.md) and [Dyia](dyia.md). This origin follows the author's latest correction. She hums songs but does not explain their origin to her sons. She arranges Hanno's apprenticeship. Dyia steps aside when the household and her wheel are seized. She uses Ravash, undergoes Hanno's experimental treatment, and recovers. [Sinna](virello.md) later deceives her into taking potent Ravash disguised as a stronger remedy, killing her.

The earlier draft describes Bakhtari ancestry through [Rukhsana](rukhsana.md) and [Iskandar](iskandar.md), with songs linked to that highland legend. Preserve this as an earlier lineage claim awaiting reconciliation with her adopted Lysandrian origin. It does not establish that she was born in or emigrated from Bakhtar, that Lysandria is part of Bakhtar, or that the song originated where she learned it.

## Draft developments — 2026-09-06

In [Chapter One](../../src/content/chapters/01-market-awnings.ts), she recalls learning pottery on Lysandria at a shared bench whose window framed both mountains. Her memory includes a fellow potter, borrowed tools, and meal breaks: work and companionship that existed before her sons' needs. The brief recollection does not establish why she left, the other potter's identity, or the origin of her song.

After eviction she occupies a borrowed back room; Hanno knows she has moved but learns Dyia's role later. [Chapter Five](../../src/content/chapters/05-the-cure.ts) gives her the choice to stop and later restart experimental treatment after explanation, with follow-up care continuing after improvement. She receives an outside pottery order and insists that Hanno record the firing money as a loan. In [Six](../../src/content/chapters/06-shadows-and-accusations.ts), she makes five bowls for an olive seller, rejects the sixth as substandard, and wants independent earnings and privacy. Sinna exploits his genuine teaching history and her wish to finish treatment; she records his visit and supposed remedy. Her death follows deception, not a chosen return to Ravash. Her finished bowls survive; Hanno later gives one to Dyia.

## Interpretation

Her work and arrangement of the apprenticeship show practical initiative under pressure. Her quiet disclosure that Dyia was at the door breaks the brothers' preferred accounts of themselves. An incomplete account of her past need not hide a royal destiny. Addiction is part of her experience, not the explanation of her entire character.

## Proposed development

Give her a professional standard, customers, and another potter with whom she exchanges help or competes. After the cure, let her want something her sons cannot agree to provide: independent earnings, privacy, or a return journey. Give her words about the risks and burden of being Hanno's first patient. If her ancestry matters, connect it to an actual obligation or remembered practice before connecting it to power.

Develop her Lysandrian ties through the former workmate's own livelihood: a letter about a shared kiln, a disputed order, or an invitation to teach could give Amara a choice independent of Hanno's training there. She might want to return for professional work without wanting to leave Carthara permanently. These are alternatives to develop, not events already established. The workmate need not know Alethea or the general.

## Open questions and continuity

Why did she leave Lysandria, and which ties does she maintain there? Does she know or accept the earlier Bakhtari genealogy, and what relationship, if any, connects that history to her island upbringing? Amara and Alethea sharing a place of origin does not make them relatives or acquaintances. Hanno now knows his mother's origin in Chapter One; his later arrival and letters should reflect that knowledge without turning his entire journey into a search for ancestry.

The borrowed room and resumed hand-built pottery now answer the immediate housing/craft questions; the host's terms and her wider professional relationships remain open. The early sheet leaves her alive after recovery and says Sinna originally supplied Ravash to bind Hanno; that is not established by the revised chapters. Her later murder must not erase the recovery or imply she caused her own death by failing morally.
