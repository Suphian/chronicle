# General Phylios

ID: `phylios` · Leader of Lysandria · Alive in his last narrated appearance

## Established in current draft

Phylios is grey-eyed, stern, and fair. He rules [Lysandria](../places/lysandria.md), the island with two enormous mountains, and values self-mastery. [Hanno](hanno.md) spends five years there in medical and alchemical study and paid work, with occasional basic self-defense practice. Phylios's interest in him concerns his knowledge and conduct, rather than exceptional fighting ability.

When Phylios's young son falls ill, the household healers keep the child alive and maintain case records. Hanno builds on their work, helps develop another approach, and works overnight with them; the boy recovers. Phylios presents **Zaharaz, an ordinary staff**, publicly in gratitude. Its importance lies in the gift and what Hanno makes of that recognition; it is not evidence of magical power or a martial rank.

Sources: Chapter [Four](../../src/content/chapters/04-gift-of-lysandria.ts), `ancient-guardians` and `the-spear`; [`world.ts`](../../src/content/world.ts), `lysandria`; [`codex.ts`](../../src/content/codex.ts).

## Interpretation

He is evidence against Hanno's claim that all authority is corrupt. Fairness should not mean that he has no competing loyalties: saving his son creates personal gratitude that may later conflict with his responsibility as ruler. His teachings matter most when they constrain his own actions.

## Proposed development

Place him in a dispute between people who train for war and people who supply them. If he learns of the Dust, a demand for evidence before punishing Hanno could be both principled and painfully self-interested. Develop the son as a person with an age and wishes before using him as a diplomatic link or hostage.

## Open questions and continuity

What makes a general the ruler, and who can oppose him lawfully? What does he know about Hanno after departure? The son's name, illness, and present age are unspecified. Zaharaz keeps its established name and the legacy scene ID `the-spear`, but the former double-bladed weapon and years of warrior training are superseded. The earlier palindrome claim is also superseded; no invented script or etymology is needed to defend it.
