# Master Sinna

ID: `virello` · Draft endpoint: Chapter Ten · Dead

The author's current name is **Sinna**, exactly this spelling. **Virello** is a superseded repository name; the filename, ID, and existing links retain `virello` for compatibility. **Virilio** was the conversational reference to the same character, not another person. Historical source snapshots retain their original wording.

## Established in current draft

A Cartharan apothecary who trains [Hanno](hanno.md), appropriates his discoveries, and dispatches him for ingredients. He understands alchemical precision well enough to teach it. When Hanno's independent cure eclipses him, Sinna murders [Amara](amara.md) with disguised Ravash and spreads accusations against the cure and its maker. Hanno baits him with a supposedly valuable shipment; Sinna steals it and dies demonstrating it publicly.

## Draft developments — 2026-09-06

The first prose pass preserves genuine competence: [One](../../src/content/chapters/01-market-awnings.ts) shows careful teaching and the limits of an experiment; Two develops the appropriation of Hanno's work. In [Six](../../src/content/chapters/06-shadows-and-accusations.ts), Amara remembers an actual past kindness. Sinna exploits that trust and her desire to finish treatment. Her record, retained contents, the apprentice's account of preparing a label, and Sinna's denial make Hanno's accusation evidence-based without constituting an uncontested prosecution.

The public demonstration is a professional gamble under pressure: preliminary examinations do not establish safety, clients are leaving, and he has promised the result before waiting for adequate observation. Hanno recognizes that familiar pattern and knowingly accepts the danger to others if Sinna does not act as expected. The inquiry exposes theft and misrepresentation; Alethea's separate clinical review restores confidence in the cure. No chemical mechanism or practical poison recipe is established.

## Adopted historical influence: Ibn Sina

The author chose Sinna with **Ibn Sina / Avicenna**, the medieval physician and philosopher who died in 1037, in mind. The [research and adaptation brief](../ideas/sinna-and-ibn-sina.md) separates the historical evidence from the fictional character.

Chapter Two now establishes three connected traits: Sinna organizes a substantial medical compendium and reads logic and natural philosophy alongside medicine; he teaches Hanno to distinguish a patient's testimony, direct observation, and a proposed explanation; and he refuses to treat an easier-to-take preparation as proof that the underlying illness has been cured. These are useful intellectual habits dramatized through original fictional encounters.

His scholarship and teaching do not excuse the later exploitation or murder. Those wrongs belong to **Sinna's fictional biography**, not Ibn Sina's life or an inference about the historical scholar's character. No passage from the historical *Canon of Medicine* is copied into the chapter, and Sinna is not Ibn Sina transplanted into Carthara.

## Interpretation

His professional identity rests on being the indispensable master. Hanno's success threatens both income and the story Sinna tells about his own merit. He treats apprenticeship as ownership. Genuine knowledge makes the exploitation more credible; he need not be secretly incompetent to have stolen work.

## Proposed development

Follow the compendium beyond its author's reputation: who keeps it after his death, which parts remain useful, and how should Hanno acknowledge teaching he received from someone who harmed his family? A former apprentice could defend effective care while giving evidence of stolen work. These later consequences remain proposals; the manuscript's eventual owner and public fate are not settled.

## Open questions and continuity

Would Hanno's murder evidence survive a hostile hearing, and what happens to Sinna's dependent apprentice? Magistrates and practitioner review appear in the prose, but a complete system for remedy certification and death inquiries remains unwritten. The current draft does not establish Sinna as a Silken Chain officer, nor confirm the questionnaire's claim he originally introduced Amara to Ravash. His death by poisoned shipment is a deliberate revision; do not restore an earlier direct killing by accident.
