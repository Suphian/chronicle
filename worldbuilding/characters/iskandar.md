# Iskandar

ID: `iskandar` · Historical figure within the fictional world · Dead

## Established in current draft

The prologue tells of a western boy-king who conquers Bakhtar after three years of costly war and learning its songs. He marries [Rukhsana](rukhsana.md), adopts highland dress, orders another advance east, and is abandoned by his army. He remains and dies in Bakhtar; the song disagrees whether fever or a knife killed him. His line descends through Rukhsana to [Amara](amara.md) and [Hanno](hanno.md).

Sources: Chapter [Zero](../../src/content/chapters/00-the-conqueror.ts), especially `iskandar`, `rukhsana`, `the-mutiny`, and `he-stayed`; [`character.ts`](../../src/content/character.ts).

## Interpretation

The legend offers the present story an image of conquest becoming attachment and authority ending in abandonment. Its uncertain ending permits conflicting memory. That uncertainty does not mean every part of the world has already been established as unreliable, nor that Hanno knows the legend's genealogical details.

## Proposed development

Write differing accounts with identifiable speakers and interests: soldiers remembering exhaustion, local families remembering losses, a later dynasty claiming inheritance. Let learning the songs entail relationships and costs rather than make cultural understanding a technique for effortless domination. His marriage cannot erase the war that preceded it.

## Open questions and continuity

What did “taking Bakhtar” actually mean—occupied roads, tribute, alliance, or governance? Fifty thousand arrive, many die, then fifty thousand depart: literal chronology or an exaggerating song? Why does he seek another war after belonging? The date, empire, descendants, and Rukhsana's position after his death remain open. This is a fictional figure with historical echoes, not a factual biography of Alexander.
