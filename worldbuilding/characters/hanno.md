# Hanno Averroes

ID: `hanno` · Draft endpoint: Chapter Ten · Alive · Full-story spoilers

## Established in current draft

Hanno is the eldest son of [Adil](adil.md) and [Amara](amara.md), a half-elf through his mother's Bakhtari ancestry. He is 115 in the character file; the chronology around that age is not settled. [Virello](virello.md) exploits his alchemical work. Travel teaches him archery, swordsmanship, and the spear; five years in Lysandria lead to Zaharaz and marriage to [Alethea](alethea.md). He cures Ravash, then engineers Virello's death after Virello murders Amara. After Numarius's banquet he develops Elysian Dust, conceals it from Alethea, and obstructs [Dyia's](dyia.md) investigation. He finds Alethea dead, looks in a mirror, and opens the door to Dyia.

His stated faith is Aetherium Harmonia. He gardens, avoids mirrors, and needs public gratitude even while passing judgment on the city. These are specific contradictions in his behavior, not evidence that every act is deceitful.

## Interpretation

He wants control over the conditions that made his family disposable. Recognition becomes his proof that he is no longer powerless. His strongest skill is turning knowledge into exchange; his weakness is treating affection as another transaction he can secure. His moral vocabulary lets him describe punishment as restoration. The market is not collectively guilty merely because he believes it is.

He loves Dyia and Alethea, yet confuses keeping them uninformed with keeping them safe. His need is to acknowledge other people's agency and the limits of his authority, not simply to forgive Numarius. Grief at the garden does not by itself constitute restitution or redemption.

## Proposed development

Use the early questionnaire's dream of a fruit cart as a modest private ambition he repeatedly postpones. Give him a chance to fund independent treatment without controlling it; his reaction would reveal whether healing or ownership matters more. Let a trusted merchant refuse him for a reason he understands. Define limits to his money, surveillance, and skill so success requires choices rather than effortless superiority.

## Open questions and continuity

How does he discover Virello's culpability? Who actually knows what is in the Dust shipments? Where is the flower cultivated at commercial scale? What can he plausibly learn about Dyia's investigation? Resolve the 115-year age against living childhood witnesses. He has never visited Bakhtar; ancestral knowledge at the final mirror is an image in the narration, not an established revelation he received. His response to Dyia remains unwritten.
