# Three possible primary stories

Status: proposals for discussion. None of the openings, deadlines, new scenes, or outcomes below is canon. Existing source events remain available as draft material. The current repository has a prologue and ten short draft chapters; their order is a biography, but the book does not have to open at Hanno's birth. The user's intended scale is an epic with tens of chapters, without a ten-chapter cap. These engines can sustain and intersect across several arcs.

The strongest existing conflict is this: two brothers believe they can protect people through systems of harm, and each can recognize the other's corruption more easily than his own. The city should exert pressure on them through actual dependencies. Start there; earn a larger epic through consequences.

## 1. The making of a savior — a family tragedy

**Story question:** Can Hanno accept being loved without making himself indispensable, or will he destroy the people whose love he needs?

**Engine:** Follow the rise from exploited apprentice to celebrated healer. Every advance in freedom creates a new dependency: patronage, medical authority, political access, secrecy. Each major success should force an irreversible moral choice. The return and cure form the center of the book; the banquet turns private revenge into collective punishment.

**POVs:** Hanno as the main viewpoint, with sustained Alethea and Dyia chapters. Alethea's clinical or civic objective must generate events, not merely explain Hanno to the reader. Keep the conqueror legend short and situated as a story people tell, rather than an authoritative explanation of inherited destiny.

**Stakes:** The family's capacity to trust one another; patients' dependence on Hanno's remedies; the city's claim that prosperity excuses exploitation. The existing garden-and-door sequence can serve as a proposed ending, but needs a preceding chain of choices, particularly Alethea's.

**Cost and tradeoff:** Greatest reuse of the current chronological draft and strongest intimate arc. Risks a long training montage and a world populated solely to shape Hanno. Compress travels unless an event creates a relationship or obligation that returns later. Alethea's death must be evaluated for her story as well as his.

**Decision needed:** Is the main pleasure watching Hanno change, and does the author want this book to end at the door or resolve what happens there? Is the garden death to remain in the next draft?

## 2. The brother's investigation — a civic crime story

**Story question:** Can Dyia expose the new drug trade without protecting the old system that taught him what justice was?

**Proposed opening:** A Dust case has a concrete consequence Dyia cannot dismiss. Establish a patient or family as a person with a life, not a nameless body used to start his plot. The evidence threatens someone protected by the harbor, fields, or garrison. Hanno is already the admired healer; the reader can learn his guilt early, letting suspense come from choices rather than hiding the premise.

**Engine:** Each investigative success narrows Hanno's room to maneuver and exposes a compromise in Dyia's own career. Hanno's response damages a relationship or resource, creating the next lead. Alethea's clinic yields a third account of the harm, and her independent choices change what either brother can do. Set a deadline only after choosing a credible institution: for example, a proposed hearing or scheduled cargo departure, neither currently established.

**POVs:** Dyia carries the investigation; Hanno carries concealment and dependence on public esteem; Alethea carries treatment, evidence, and her own obligations. Regional viewpoint characters need independent objectives and consequential decisions, not merely information to deliver to this family. Chuluun is already available in the recovered source, subject to resolving his elder/prince depictions. Use childhood and travel history when it changes the meaning of a present decision.

**Stakes:** Whether residents receive protection or another scapegoat; whether the guard investigates its patrons; whether exposing Hanno also exposes Dyia's killings; whether Alethea's care remains possible as secrecy collapses. Existing lore about the Seats supplies competing pressures without requiring an immediate continent-spanning war.

**Cost and tradeoff:** Best immediate readability and strongest reason to consult character profiles and the city map. Requires real clues, realistic knowledge boundaries, and competent opposition. The existing “every lead dissolves” summary must become visible investigative turns. Alethea's draft ending is optional in this structure; changing it requires a recorded editorial decision.

**Decision needed:** Is Dyia the lead viewpoint, are Hanno's actions known to the reader from the start, and what public consequence will make the ending more than a private reckoning?

## 3. After the knock — a struggle over Carthara

**Story question:** Who can govern a city whose cure, wealth, and public order all depend on people implicated in its suffering?

**Proposed opening:** Resolve Dyia's choice at the door. Arrest, protection, or a conditional bargain creates different stories; choose one rather than keeping all three vaguely possible. Hanno's earlier life becomes the prehistory to a political crisis.

**Engine:** The consequences of that choice change who controls medicine, trade, evidence, and armed force. The Three Seats compete to survive, while residents and institutions respond in ways none of them fully controls. A failed alliance must affect food, wages, treatment, or access, not just who sits at a council table. External regions enter through existing dependencies before new armies are introduced.

**POVs:** Dyia and Hanno plus a carefully chosen institutional or civilian viewpoint. If the garden ending remains, Alethea cannot simply disappear from the city's memory: establish the people and obligations attached to her work before her death. If her survival becomes the preferred proposal, record the revision before drafting this sequel-shaped story.

**Stakes:** Legitimacy, material survival, and whether a change of rulers changes the conditions that made Ravash and Dust profitable. The temptation for Dyia is to justify a new coercive arrangement as temporary protection; for Hanno, to make his usefulness a shield against accountability.

**Cost and tradeoff:** Largest capacity for a broad epic, but the least supported by existing scenes. Requires decisions about the greater empire, succession, taxation, foreign dependencies, and the fate of the brothers. Risks using a whole novel's worth of emotional history as exposition before the reader cares.

**Decision needed:** What does Dyia actually do at the door, what is Hanno publicly known to have done, and is Carthara sovereign or answerable to a greater power? These cannot remain undefined while this plot advances.

## Recommendation

Develop **Option 2, Dyia's investigation, as the opening thread**, keeping Option 1 as the historical backbone and Option 3 as a possible consequence. It gives the reader a clear present-tense problem and a practical reason to explore the world: a clue has a place, a person has an obligation, a journey changes what someone can do. It also makes the brother who stayed an active co-author of the plot. The investigation is an entry into the epic, not a requirement that every region revolve around Hanno.

Begin with a short treatment and three substantial opening chapters, not a rewrite of every region. Before those chapters, establish the age chronology, Alethea's independent objective, the investigative chain, and the rule preventing each Seat from simply removing an inconvenient person. Preserve the current ending as an explicitly labeled candidate while comparing it with alternatives.

## Expandable epic architecture

The [recovered geography](sources/early-geography.md) already contains enough independent institutions and competing obligations to support regional stories. Reconcile its positions and identities using [the geography ledger](geography-reconciliation.md), then develop the following **proposed** arc structure. A planning pass might discover roughly 20–40 substantial chapters across these arcs; that is an illustrative range, neither a quota nor a ceiling. Split or combine arcs according to the choices and consequences they require. Current Chapters One–Ten can expand into multiple chapters or move into interleaved history without losing their stable source IDs.

Each arc earns chapters through a chain of decisions: objective, resistance, choice, consequence, changed situation. Use a convergence ledger recording what each region wants independently, what it offers or withholds, when another region learns of that choice, and how long the consequences take to arrive. Avoid one hidden organization causing every crisis; the recovered world is more interesting when different histories meet.

A historical event such as the user's remembered attack on a pilgrim ship could eventually enter through harbor evidence, a family's history, trade restrictions, or public memory. Its role should change someone's present decision. Keep it in the idea/research stage until the history is sourced and its connection to the chosen story is earned; do not invent a canonical atrocity merely to enlarge the setting.

## A prompt for the next story-development pass

> Develop an expandable epic treatment with Dyia's investigation as an opening thread, using the existing chapters, character files, recovered geography, and geography reconciliation. Plan in arcs that can sustain tens of substantial chapters; there is no ten-chapter cap or fixed final count. Treat current text as revisable draft, identify all source differences, and do not silently promote suggestions into canon. Keep Hanno morally responsible, make Dyia's own coercion consequential, and give Alethea a goal, relationships, and decisions independent of her marriage. Give regional viewpoints conflicts that exist without Hanno: consider Ruhania's institutions, Chuluun and the fragmented Khanate, Sidrat's sanctuary, and Solunari pilgrimage without silently combining faiths or inventing continental placement. For each chapter outline, name the viewpoint, immediate objective, opposing pressure, irreversible choice, consequence, location, and knowledge gained or lost. Show how food, debt, shipping, medicine, and enforcement constrain the choices. Flag unsupported facts and continuity problems. Offer competing solutions where an author decision matters, especially the chronology, Chuluun's identity and status, Alethea's fate, and Dyia's action at the door. End with the smallest set of decisions needed to draft the opening. Do not write a predetermined redemption or assume inherited ancestry makes Hanno exceptional.
