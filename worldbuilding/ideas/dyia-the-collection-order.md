# Dyia and the collection order

Status: **author pitch and editorial alternatives, 2026-09-07**, with the later adoption of Chuluun's teacher/resistance-leader role recorded below. The debt sequence and crop choice remain proposals; this note does not establish a new debt law or identify the fields' crop.

## The author's pitch

Paraphrase of the current conversation: Dyia feels Hanno has abandoned their family and joins the guard to pay the bills. Numarius raises the payments associated with the fields, perhaps to test Dyia's loyalty. Collectors take the family home while Dyia does nothing. The author asks whether his failure is credible: could he not intervene? Related questions concern what Numarius grows and whether it supplies Ravash, and whether Chuluun is the intended Omar Mukhtar-inspired character.

## What the draft already establishes

In [Chapter One](../../src/content/chapters/01-market-awnings.ts), `the-fields`, Idris is taken into provisional labor before either son's adult employment. In `the-apothecary`, Amara negotiates Hanno's household allowance, and apprenticeship years pass. In `ravash`, Idris's maintenance charges keep the account from shrinking; Dyia joins for regular pay, rations, and a hope of reaching officials who can hear their petition. His resentment already concerns Hanno's absence at the workshop and avoidance of difficult conversations.

In `the-door`, Dyia reads the destination beforehand, requests a different posting, says the account is under review, offers payment, and asks for time until payday. The sergeant refuses and makes continued employment conditional on stepping aside. Dyia chooses the post. The shopkeeper protects some bowls and offers Amara a back room; she preserves personal possessions and continues working by hand. Dyia leaves with the detail. He fails to stop the eviction, but he does not literally make no attempt.

In `the-sentencing`, a later hearing confirms Idris's continued labor. Hanno knows Amara has moved, but not that Dyia was in the eviction detail. [Chapter Two](../../src/content/chapters/02-bitter-apprenticeship.ts), `something-breaking`, reveals that participation to Hanno. In `go-then`, Dyia argues another guard would have enforced the order if he had refused. Hanno's actual departure comes in [Chapter Three](../../src/content/chapters/03-journeys.ts), `sent-away`, after he obtains a written commitment that money will continue reaching Amara.

The [guard](../factions/the-city-guard.md) belongs to Vael's command. Numarius benefits from its debt enforcement; an instruction from his estate must still reach it through some administrative and command process. That process has not been fully written.

## Credibility and the recommended revision

**Interpretation:** Dyia's choice is credible for a new guard with little money or authority. It needs the distinction between being unable to cancel an order and choosing not to oppose it at personal cost. If every possible action is made useless, the scene absolves him in advance; if he can casually save the home, preserving his job becomes difficult to understand as family protection.

**Recommended proposal:** expand his attempt into a small sequence before the arrival. Let him take an actual wage figure and the family's receipts to an official. He wins something limited, such as an appointment to review the account, and mistakes that appointment for protection against enforcement. Amara wants written confirmation that the seizure is delayed; he reassures her before obtaining it. At the door, the sergeant produces an order that has not been suspended. Dyia discovers the difference between access and authority when it is too late to conceal it from her.

Give him one costly, achievable action after he cannot save the rooms. For example, he can insist that Amara's objection and the wheel's disputed value be entered into the inventory, delaying the detail and exposing his conflict of interest. The proposed failure is that he relinquishes even that narrower demand when threatened with dismissal, promising himself he will correct the record later. The existing hearing then has a sharper cause: his mother must challenge a valuation he helped allow to pass. This procedure and his omission would need explicit prose; neither is current fact.

Keep his other contributions real. He can bring food and wages, seek the clerk, and love Amara while making this choice. Keep her objection specific: she needed her means of earning preserved and an honest warning, not his assurance that a uniform would eventually fix everything. Hanno's accusation remains incomplete too: his allowance matters, but so do the evenings he chooses the bench over her questions. Both brothers seek recognition for useful work while overlooking different needs.

## Is this Numarius's loyalty test?

**Not established.** The draft gives a sergeant a direct obedience test at the door. It does not show Numarius increasing this family's balance in order to test an otherwise unimportant recruit. A personalized test needs a reason Numarius knows Dyia, wants his particular service, and expects this act to prove something useful.

Two proposed alternatives:

- **Recommended: ordinary extraction, enforced obedience.** Numarius's estate imposes or maintains charges across debtor accounts for revenue and labor control. The sergeant knowingly keeps Dyia on the detail because he will not grant a family exception. Numarius's material interest causes the harm without requiring him to choreograph every family encounter. Any new charge and its timing must be specified.
- **More personal version: a bargain that later becomes leverage.** An estate intermediary first helps Dyia obtain a review or a limited concession, then demands cooperation when another payment falls due. This requires earlier scenes showing why the intermediary cultivates him, how Vael's guard receives the assignment, and what refusal costs. It should not depend on an unexplained promise of future captaincy or make all the Seats one perfectly coordinated conspiracy.

## Chronology and accounting constraints

The current maintenance charge predates Dyia joining the guard. If the proposed escalation follows recruitment, it must be a subsequent charge, reassessment, or withdrawal of a concession; it cannot be described as the original reason Idris was taken. The account is attached to coerced labor and the family's old debt, not an established lease under which they voluntarily rent Numarius's farm.

Hanno is still in Carthara at the eviction. His earlier emotional absence can motivate Dyia without moving the travel forward. Moving the eviction after Hanno leaves would require rewriting the hearing, the Chapter Two discovery and quarrel, Amara's housing, remittances, and the departure. Preserve Hanno's knowledge boundary unless that larger revision is deliberately chosen. The long interval between provisional labor and formal sentencing, the family's liability for maintenance, wages, and the seized household's legal status still need definition.

## The fields: crop, importance, and Ravash

**Established:** the [fields](../places/numarius-fields.md) are east of Carthara and use coerced labor. [Chapter Seven](../../src/content/chapters/07-rising-influence.ts), `courtesies`, explicitly gives Numarius stored grain, harbor repairs dependent on grain duties, and Vael demanding food for the garrison. The field profile still leaves the precise crop open. Stored grain supports an agricultural basis for his power; it does not prove the species or that every stored load grew on his land.

**Established connection to Ravash:** [Chapter Five](../../src/content/chapters/05-the-cure.ts), `seeds-of-conflict`, says all three Seats profit from the Silken Chain's trade. It also distinguishes improved labor and commerce after treatment from the lost illicit income. No current scene establishes Ravash being grown in Numarius's fields.

**Recommended proposal:** make a staple grain, with wheat as a candidate, the principal field crop. This gives Numarius power over food, rents, debt, labor, and the supplies the other Seats need. His Ravash income can remain an additional corrupt interest; the whole economy need not secretly grow one drug. Before adoption, define water access or reserves that explain why he retains power through the drought that ruins Idris, the harvest cycle, and how crop sales relate to labor accounts. A dedicated Ravash plot or raw-material contract is an available alternative, but needs evidence, cultivation requirements, and a reason its discovery has not already exposed the trade.

## Chuluun and the teacher

**Later author adoption:** the **Omar Mukhtar-inspired teacher and resistance-leader role belongs to [Chuluun](../characters/chuluun.md)**. The earlier [separate upland character proposal](the-teacher-of-the-uplands.md) is superseded. Chuluun remains the displaced Leonin elder in the Tengeri camps, keeping records and working under compulsion. Chapter Three now adds teaching, former students and fighters, a limited household mandate, and a supply dispute in which his authority is challenged.

**Still open:** his fuller command history, relationships with the named Leonin factions, the authorities' knowledge of his work, and any future capture or death. The adopted role does not settle the old prince/elder contradiction, make him leader of every camp, or import a historical ending. The crop recommendation above remains separately **proposed**: wheat or another staple grain has not been adopted, and no Ravash crop has been established.
