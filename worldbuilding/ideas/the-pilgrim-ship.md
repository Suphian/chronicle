# The pilgrim ship

Status: **PROPOSED — not canon**  
Origin: the author's recalled account of Portuguese ships attacking a wealthy Hajj vessel.  
Recommended placement: expand the years abroad in **Chapter Three, “Journeys into the Unknown.”**  
Scope: a possible maritime strand; no existing chapter or character history has been changed.

## The historical starting point

The likely reference is the **Miri**, also rendered **Mirim**, a Muslim pilgrim ship attacked by Vasco da Gama's Portuguese expedition off India's Malabar coast in **1502**. The passengers were Muslim pilgrims; the Portuguese were the attackers. The ship was plundered and burned. This belonged to a wider campaign to impose Portuguese power on existing Indian Ocean trade. [World History Encyclopedia, “Vasco da Gama”](https://www.worldhistory.org/Vasco_da_Gama/); [University of Kent thesis, discussion of the Miri](https://kar.kent.ac.uk/id/document/3223875).

The near-contemporary **Calcoen**, translated by J. Ph. Berjeau in 1874, reports 380 men plus women and children, at least 12,000 ducats and additional goods, and dates the burning to 1 October. Those are the account's claims, not audited totals. Its introduction notes that later historians reported children being spared, unlike its own account. Accordingly, “everybody was killed” should not be stated as settled fact. The exact date, passenger total, and number of survivors require comparison across accounts; modern retellings also disagree about whether the pilgrims were going to or returning from Mecca. The remembered wealth is plausible, but “a ship full of gold” overstates what we can establish here. [Calcoen, printed p. 33; introduction pp. 10–11](https://www.sabrizain.org/malaya/library/calcoendutc.pdf).

These European accounts preserve evidence of violence but are not neutral descriptions of Muslim lives. The fictional development below is a proposal, not a reconstruction of the historical passengers.

## Recommended story: the missing voyage

During Hanno's years collecting ingredients, a merchant vessel carrying pilgrims disappears from a sea route linked to Carthara. News arrives through several channels: an overdue cargo account, a relative searching the harbor, and testimony gathered by a charitable institution. Hanno meets people already organizing relief and demanding an investigation. Their work gives him something harder than a heroic rescue: an opportunity to help without being its center.

Establish the pilgrims' destination and faith before drafting. **Sidrat Al Muntaha is currently a land of sacred lakes, not an established pilgrimage destination.** Do not quietly convert Lake Siraj into Mecca or copy Hajj rites into an undefined fantasy religion. Decide whether this world contains Islam explicitly or draws on researched institutional and cultural histories through distinct fictional traditions.

A proposed charitable endowment could fund lodging, medicine, and passage for travelers. Give its administrators disagreements over evidence, compensation, and whether to suspend voyages. Show the community before and after the loss: teaching, marriages, navigation, trading partnerships, care for elders, ordinary humor. A passenger might also be a scholar, craftswoman, broker, or parent; pilgrimage is part of a life, not a substitute for one. Develop at least two people from this community with independent goals before writing the attack.

## Three connections to the existing story

1. **Hanno and responsibility.** His ingredient shipments already cross long distances. A delayed consignment can introduce the crisis naturally. Early Hanno can contribute practical alchemy and transport knowledge while resenting the limits of his help. Later, his use of merchants who do not know they carry Elysian Dust echoes a question he once understood: what does a trader owe the people downstream? This deepens his contradiction without making the massacre the explanation for all his later choices.
2. **Corvo and the harbor.** Corvo controls customs and shipping. Let her face a choice over publishing records, releasing relief cargo, or financing escorts. Her incentive to protect revenue supplies conflict without making her secretly responsible for this attack too. The foreign perpetrators need their own command structure, objectives, and accountability.
3. **Dyia and jurisdiction.** Dyia might receive relatives' petitions in Carthara while his brother is away. His authority stops at the docks; his instinct is to find someone punishable. A dispute over detaining an innocent foreign sailor can test his drift toward collective punishment. No sudden naval command or investigative expertise should appear without preparation.

## What must hold together

Map the route, ports, travel times, seasonal sailing window, fresh-water capacity, and means of transmitting news. Explain why escorts were absent and what naval advantage enabled the attack. If magic or futuristic technology exists here, establish why it cannot cheaply rescue everyone or guarantee safe passage. Trace losses through wages, food prices, credit, bereaved households, and political pressure; stolen wealth must have owners and uses.

An alternative is an earlier disaster remembered through a port institution and a contested compensation claim. That needs less change to Chapter Three. A later independent novella could center the maritime community directly. Neither requires every major character to have witnessed the same catastrophe.

Next decision: choose the community, its faith, and one person whose life matters beyond their connection to Hanno. Then test the proposed route and chronology before promoting any element into canon.
