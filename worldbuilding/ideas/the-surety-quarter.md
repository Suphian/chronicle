# The Surety Quarter: Leonin displacement and enslavement

Status: **the Leonin premise is adopted; the Cartharan extension remains proposed.** The author's clarification places Chuluun and the lion-folk at the center of this strand. Their homeland has been destroyed; the Tengeri Wastes contain refugee camps subjected to recurring bombardment, coerced labor, and dehumanizing propaganda. Chuluun is part of the enslaved community, not an outside observer.

The proposed Surety Quarter could receive displaced Leonin households and labor detainees, connecting their experience to Carthara. The quarter, route, local rules, and additional characters below remain **proposed**. The earlier Ruhaniyya migrant community remains a **distinct proposal**, not a replacement identity for the Leonin and not already canon.

## Original pitch and later direction

The initial comparison is preserved:

> similar to Indians and H1B1 people, or like they are immigrants or asylum seekers, and they're kind of like the slave labor of a particular region.

The later clarification identifies Chuluun and the Leonin as the central group, including Chuluun being “actually part of that slave group.” The following summarizes the subsequent author direction rather than inventing additional quotations:

- The Leonin are lion-people whose homeland has been destroyed. Refugee camps in the Tengeri Wastes are central to their present experience.
- Enslavement and compelled labor are actual conditions, not metaphors for an unfavorable job. Chuluun himself lives within that coercion.
- Camp residents face regular bombardment and dehumanization. Official propaganda brands resistance as terrorism.
- Numarius and his people belong to the opposing power. Their people were themselves initially welcomed as refugees after losing their homeland and later dispossessed their Leonin hosts. The founding generation and the present lord's personal chronology remain to be distinguished.
- Leonin live in a worldwide diaspora. They carry the lost homeland's flag, which may be a public banner, private cloth, or small emblem; possession does not settle an individual's politics.
- Leonin responses differ. Working faction profiles distinguish camp representation and release claims, negotiated peace, armed resistance that rejects surrender, and diaspora aid and testimony.

The [Leonin profile](../factions/the-leonin.md), [Chuluun](../characters/chuluun.md), [Tengeri Wastes](../places/tengeri-wastes.md), and [Chapter Three](../../src/content/chapters/03-journeys.ts) carry that narrative integration. The core premise is adopted even while dates, borders, institutions, and the extent of a particular character's knowledge are being reconciled.

## What the strand is about

**How do displaced people preserve a life, resist enslavement, and establish what is being done to them when the authorities control movement and the public account?**

Hanno meets people seeking food, teaching children, making music, repairing things, finding relatives, and deciding how to resist. These lives precede his arrival and continue after it. His technical help, purchases, or willingness to carry testimony can matter without making him the liberator of an entire people.

A paper describing an assignment as voluntary is a claim the story tests against a person's actual ability to refuse. A report denying civilians were present at a bombarded site cannot erase the civilians encountered there. Keep propaganda attributed to its speaker; the narration should not adopt a designation merely because the powerful repeat it.

Armed resistance does not turn every resident into a combatant. People can support liberation, oppose particular tactics, want to evacuate, or fear reprisals without forfeiting their claim to freedom. Their disagreements do not make bombardment of households a neutral difference of opinion.

The history of Numarius's people also needs precise responsibility. The earlier welcome and subsequent dispossession are adopted. It does not follow that every member of that population participated equally, that its descendants all endorse the present policy, or that its own refugee history excuses what its institutions now do.

## A community before and beyond victimhood

These details are **proposed development**, not universal traits of lion-folk:

- Residents remember work as builders, teachers, potters, water specialists, herders, performers, traders, and clerks. Captivity interrupts those occupations without making physical strength their only useful quality.
- A shelter's evening lessons include comic stories, arithmetic, repaired instruments, and disputed versions of old songs. Younger people who know the homeland through other people's memories can ask unwelcome questions about the life being promised to them.
- Every Leonin carries the lost-home flag, but uses can differ: a hidden scrap in a work coat, an openly worn emblem, a ceremonial banner. A negotiator, a militant, and somebody chiefly trying to reunite a household may honor the same flag.
- Missing-person records belong to families as well as campaigns. Some want names published; others fear exposing relatives to those controlling labor transfers. A recorder must negotiate consent rather than turning every grief into a public resource.
- The recovered clan history supplies differences to explore. Altan, Tsetseg, the empty Golden Yurt, and the missing fifteenth tribe need a timeline in relation to destruction and displacement. Do not automatically turn every older mystery into another result of the same event.
- Leonin differ in class, belief, gender, age, disability, profession, and allegiance. Enslavement is imposed by institutions, not caused by Leonin nature. Claims that lion-folk are inherently dangerous or suited to servitude belong to the dehumanizing account being challenged.

The working factions should remain distinguishable: **Return Councils** pursue camp representation, releases, and home claims; **Open Hand** seeks negotiated peace with Numarius; **Last Standard** rejects surrender and supports armed resistance; **Scattered Hearths** organizes diaspora aid, testimony, and work rights. Membership, overlap, local branches, and individual disagreements need scenes. None automatically speaks for everyone bearing the flag.

## Proposed Cartharan receiving district

The **Surety Quarter** would be a neighborhood named for an administrative register. Leonin households arrive through displacement, evacuation, release, family ties, or coercive transfer. Contractors seek work for harbor maintenance, water systems, construction, storage, and fields. A shared connection to Numarius's power makes this plausible, but the specific labor route and receiving machinery still need to be written.

The book must retain distinctions officials find convenient to blur:

| Situation | Proposed experience in Carthara | Distinction to preserve |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Enslaved Leonin transferred under a claim of control | An agency or contractor directs work, prevents departure, retains documents, or transfers control to another employer. | An allowance or a paper called a placement agreement does not make captivity consensual. Recognizing or refusing the claim is a political conflict, not a settled local law yet. |
| Leonin refugees seeking safety | A household arrives separately and needs lodging, work, care, or a protection hearing. | A refugee is not necessarily enslaved at this moment or politically aligned with every other resident. Refugee status is not proof of armed activity. |
| Leonin with release documents or established residence | People can fund relief, translate, employ neighbors, and make claims with more leverage. | Their greater security does not require gratitude, unanimity, or speaking on behalf of every camp. |
| Proposed Ruhaniyya workers and protection-seekers | A different community arrives through recruitment, family connections, and separate protection needs. | They are not renamed Leonin and do not inherit the destruction of the Leonin homeland as their default history. |
| Cartharan-born residents and employers | Households share markets, work, and political interests with arrivals. Some offer help, some profit, some disagree. | Responsibility attaches to actions and institutions; there are no two internally uniform ethnic sides. |

A residents' assembly could call its shared courtyard the **Open Table**. Leonin residents should shape its priorities and rules rather than appear only as recipients of other people's generosity. Meals, translation, childcare, rent, and witnesses cost money and labor. Who pays, who works unpaid, and who votes are useful disagreements.

## How captivity could be administered

These mechanics are **proposed**, while the underlying Leonin enslavement is adopted:

1. Camp authorities issue labor-transfer papers, restrict movement, and demand work under credible threats. Chuluun's own enforced account and the records kept by households make the coercion visible.
2. An intermediary claims that transport, food, or protection creates an entitlement to control labor. A Cartharan contractor seeks recognition of that claim. The transferred people have not thereby consented to become property.
3. A worker can receive an allowance and walk along a public street while detention threats, forced return, retained papers, or consequences for relatives prevent departure. Show the actual constraint rather than treating an unlocked door as proof of freedom.
4. The same office also processes ordinary sponsored work. Mixing those records lets an official describe every arrival as a willing employee. Comparing conditions exposes the false equivalence without implying that every sponsored job is slavery.
5. Residents demand release from coercive claims, withheld earnings, family contact, and safe residence. **Changing the name of the person who controls their labor is not release.** A sympathetic offer to buy a claim must answer whether the person can then leave and refuse further work.
6. After release, food, shelter, treatment, income, and protection from recapture still matter. Disagreement with a benefactor is not ingratitude that forfeits freedom.

The inheritance, duration, transfer, and local legal recognition of enslavement need deliberate decisions. Neither the status of children nor the reach of camp authorities abroad should emerge accidentally from a metaphor about debt.

## Beneficiaries and limits

**Numarius:** his people's dispossession of the Leonin is part of the adopted history. The connection between the current lord's fields, overseas or regional authorities, and particular labor contractors needs a clear chain of responsibility. Keep Leonin captivity distinct from the local debtor process that took [Adil](../characters/adil.md); similar coercion does not establish identical legal status.

**Corvo:** harbor contractors may profit from transfers and permits. She also needs skilled maintenance and reliable trade. Her [existing control of customs](../characters/corvo.md) does not automatically establish authorship of bombardments or complete knowledge of every contractor's practices.

**Vael and Dyia:** the guard may receive an order to enforce a transfer or detain someone described as an escaped worker. [Dyia](../characters/dyia.md) can decide a concrete case without acquiring jurisdiction over the entire system. His choice needs consequences for the person seeking safety, not only his own conscience.

**Contractors:** a small firm squeezed by unpaid invoices is still responsible for coercing an employee. Some know what they are buying; others choose not to examine the claim. Staff can preserve documents, testify, or refuse instructions without their employer becoming mysteriously powerless.

**Leonin residents:** organizing records, care, work, representation, and alliances is part of the plot. The Return Councils and Scattered Hearths can disagree about scarce funds; Open Hand can contest a tactic proposed by Last Standard. A collective mistake does not erase the legitimacy of the demand to end captivity.

## Three candidate scenes

### 1. Names before numbers — Chuluun

**Possible place in the book:** expand Tengeri within Part II of the [overall outline](../story/overall-outline.md), coordinating with the revised [Chapter Three](../../src/content/chapters/03-journeys.ts) rather than duplicating its events.

Chuluun tries to reconcile missing-person and labor-assignment records while a neighbor insists a relative's name has been misspelled. A lesson and a repair take place nearby. Bombardment interrupts ordinary work. Afterward an official account describes the struck facilities as hostile and the transferred workers as volunteers.

**Turn:** preserving testimony creates a responsibility toward the people named. Hanno can witness, copy, or carry a consented statement; he is not the only person capable of proving civilians existed. Chuluun and the households decide how the record may be used, with different factions pressing different priorities.

### 2. A name on the wrong paper — Ivara

**Possible place in the book:** an added Cartharan chapter during the practice-growth arc. Establish the transfer route and elapsed time first.

Ivara seeks an explanation of the paper attached to her assignment. Her contractor expects her to acknowledge a claim over work she cannot refuse. The clerk calls it routine placement. A camp record gives her a name and history the receiving account omits.

**Turn:** she refuses the acknowledgment and records an objection. Nayra's professional dispute can make her an ally, but a delayed transfer between voluntary jobs is visibly different from Ivara's captivity. Alethea can provide treatment or testimony while respecting Ivara's control over her account. Hanno may offer to purchase the claim; Ivara demands release and independence rather than a kinder master.

### 3. What a release must mean — Rashet

Rashet helps compare transfer names with people present in Carthara. A proposed hearing offers another contractor while preserving the claims over their labor. An organizer wants every record published in retaliation. Rashet insists that both settlement and publication require the affected people's consent.

**Recommended outcome, still proposed:** a defined group gains release from coercive claims, possession of documents, an account of withheld earnings, and a separate protection hearing. Residents organize temporary lodging and voluntary work. This does not free everybody in Tengeri, guarantee permanent safety, or end bombardment. It establishes people with evidence and collective power that the authorities must confront.

**Consequence:** others demand the same release. Contractors dispute the precedent; camp authorities challenge the records; the Open Table confronts its financial limits. Ivara and Rashet continue making choices after Hanno's involvement ends. Their lives do not conclude when he has learned something.

## Ruhania and other strands remain distinct

The initial version proposed a migrant neighborhood using [Ruhania's existing regional diversity](../sources/early-geography.md): Al-Rutbah and Zulimar, Qa'hir and Qalb Al-Sahra, Safar, and coastal towns. That remains proposed material. A flood-marking gathering, multilingual theater, bookbinding, mutual aid, and debates over sponsorship could give it a separate life. These inventions do not describe an entire real nationality and do not convert Ruhaniyya people into Leonin.

Both migration and labor-transfer routes need the [geography reconciliation](../geography-reconciliation.md). One route does not establish the other. The worldwide Leonin diaspora is adopted; its particular communities, institutions, and paths still need to be drawn through evidence and deliberate invention.

The [teacher of the uplands](the-teacher-of-the-uplands.md) could connect through a school, refuge, or a request for civilian relief. It is not automatically the same occupation, faction, or campaign. Residents may support aid while rejecting a military levy. The [pilgrim-ship proposal](the-pilgrim-ship.md) could connect through passage records or an endowment; it does not explain every displacement or loss.

Religious life can include teaching, hospitality, arbitration, charity, and disagreement over public duty. Do not infer one faith for all Leonin, Ruhaniyya, or resistance supporters. Islamic historical inspiration is a source of researched possibilities, not proof that all existing traditions form one religion or that every crisis has one concealed author.

## Research notes from the initial comparison

These distinctions preserve accuracy in the original real-world comparison. They do not weaken or reclassify the adopted fictional enslavement premise.

**Nationality and employment categories:** “Indian” is a nationality, not a visa status. H-1B is a U.S. temporary-work category associated with specialty occupations; H-1B1 is a distinct category for qualifying professionals from **Chile and Singapore**. The likely intended initial reference was H-1B. These are not definitions of slavery. [U.S. Department of State, temporary worker categories](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/employment/temporary-worker-visas.html).

**Protection claims:** an asylum-seeker seeks international protection and may await determination. A refugee meets an applicable refugee definition because return would expose them to specified threats such as persecution; recognition acknowledges that condition rather than creating it. International and regional definitions differ. Neither term determines a person's occupation or makes forced labor permissible. [UNHCR, asylum-seekers](https://www.unhcr.org/about-unhcr/who-we-protect/asylum-seekers), [UNHCR glossary](https://www.unhcr.org/glossary).

**Forced labor:** the ILO identifies involuntary work combined with a threatened penalty. Consent at recruitment does not settle whether someone remains free to leave. Coercive debt, seized documents, and threats can matter. H-1B workers have labor protections, including wage and anti-retaliation protections; fictional forced transfers are not descriptions of their legal condition. [ILO definition](https://www.ilo.org/topics/forced-labour-modern-slavery-and-trafficking-persons/what-forced-labour), [ILO, coercion and related terms](https://www.ilo.org/resource/article/meanings-forced-labour), [U.S. Department of Labor, H-1B worker protections](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/workers/h1b).
