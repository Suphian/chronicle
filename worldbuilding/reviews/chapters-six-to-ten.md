# First prose pass: Chapters Six–Ten

Status: editorial record of the authorized first narrative pass. This records changes made in the prose, not a list of independently approved immutable world facts. The author can revise the story after reading it. Chapter count remains expandable.

## Scope and form

Replaced the compressed scene summaries in Chapters Six–Ten with sustained present-tense narrative. Every original chapter slug, order, and scene ID survives. Added `the-clinic-ledger` to Chapter Seven for Alethea's independent clinic negotiation. Scenes identify their close-third viewpoint with `pov`; there are no perspective changes within a scene. Prose counts after review repairs: Six 2,203; Seven 2,131; Eight 2,174; Nine 2,334; Ten 2,455. Total: 11,297 words.

These chapters are a first prose draft, not a finished epic. Six in particular could later become several chapters: Amara's resumed livelihood and death; an inquiry and professional backlash; the revenge. The present pass preserves the existing architecture so each source scene remains traceable.

## Preserved continuity

- Dyia retains the author's exact spelling. No brother is renamed or added.
- Amara recovers before Sinna deceives and murders her. Her death is not a relapse, a failed cure, or a punishment for wanting privacy.
- Hanno kills Sinna through the deliberately prepared stolen shipment; he does not kill him directly with the spear.
- Numarius humiliates Hanno with Adil's fate at the banquet and orders further debtor labor. Hanno creates Elysian Dust afterward and conceals his role from Alethea and Dyia.
- The Dust reaches influential households and harms people beyond Hanno's intended targets. The Three Seats are neither omniscient nor responsible for every individual act.
- Alethea develops dependence and dies in the garden. Chapter Ten ends with Hanno opening the door and finding Dyia. It chooses no arrest, protection, bargain, redemption, or final political outcome.

## Substantive additions and repairs

**Amara's agency and the murder inquiry:** An outside olive seller commissions twelve hand-built bowls; Amara completes five. This develops Chapter Five's external pottery order. She wants paid work and privacy rather than life as her son's permanent first patient. Sinna uses his genuine history as a teacher and occasional healer to make his apparent kindness credible. She records his delivery. Hanno and Alethea retain and compare the bottle with their own samples; Sinna's apprentice supplies limited corroboration but fears losing his bed and employment. Hanno's conviction rests on these facts and his knowledge of Sinna, not on omniscient access to the murderer's mind. The material is not presented as a completed prosecution.

**Sinna's gamble and Hanno's responsibility:** The self-demonstration grows from lost clients, promised discovery, and premature reliance on preliminary examinations that do not establish safety. Hanno consciously accepts the risk that a merchant, apprentice, or guest could encounter the lethal material instead. A guest's request to try it makes that risk visible. The story contains no usable poison preparation. Sinna's theft is exposed; Alethea's continued record-sharing and independent review restore trust in the cure. His death alone does not prove Hanno's medicine safe.

**A clinic with authority of its own:** Alethea negotiates a separate lease/account, admission authority, and support from paying patients and the workshop. The clinic has an unnamed steward and cooperating practitioners. She and Hanno disagree over material authority while retaining tenderness. Her independent records create a principled boundary against both her husband's requests and Dyia's broad inquiry. Contingency transfers and authorized supply payments let the institution continue after her death.

**Power with material limits:** Carthara's expansion requires builders and trained assistants; Hanno cannot dictate an arch's load or immediately scale good medicine. The Seats bargain over grain, berth repairs, and garrison provision. Numarius offers field access while reserving the right to overrule clinical decisions. The banquet follows Hanno's refusal to endorse that arrangement. Numarius uses a protected public setting to reassert the hierarchy, making his calculated risk more specific than cruelty alone.

**Hanno's turn is chosen and delayed:** He notices residents who were absent from the banquet before suppressing their differences in his judgment of the city. Dust production requires additional botanical purchases and propagation through failed plants and a growing season. No quantities, cultivation techniques, or recipes are supplied. The broker knowingly sells Dust; ordinary carriers are not necessarily informed. Hanno presents himself as financier, but cannot know what the broker suspects. Warned that wealthy households cannot contain the substance's reach, he proceeds anyway.

**An investigation that can progress:** Dyia's murdered suspect leaves a sister seeking property and an unanswered chain of questions. His previous violence cost evidence as well as lives. Later he releases wrongly detained chair carriers, seeks bounded records, asks for refusals in writing, and builds a lead through ordinary accounts. These improvements do not erase his killings. Hanno learns only parts of the inquiry through requests that reach people connected to his business; he pays to end agreements and conceal evidence without controlling every investigator or witness.

**Alethea's dependence and death:** The prose explicitly identifies self-testing as an unsafe personal rationalization, not normal healer practice or a reliable experiment. Relief becomes repeated use, concealment, missed work, attempted cessation, and difficulty applying to herself the help she offers patients. She eventually discloses to the steward, delegates care, completes a letter, and arranges an appointment. She declines the offer to remain at the clinic and goes home intending to speak with Hanno. The text does not establish an intent to die, a complete account of her last hours, or that every question she had about the Elysian plant was resolved. Hanno cannot know her final thoughts. Her plans and relationships persist beyond his realization.

## Unresolved work

- Absolute ages and chronology remain open. Added intervals use seasons and relative elapsed time, not a chosen calendar or a solution to Hanno's stated age of 115.
- Chapter Five supplies limited notice of Adil's death; the banquet weaponizes his specific fate. Neither chapter establishes a grave, eyewitness account of burial, or complete evidence for every part of Numarius's boast.
- Chapter Eight's `elysian-dust` retains its original Moonlit Isle association because the wedding is explicitly remembered there; physical action is in Carthara's garden/workshop. The reader/map label should identify the island as remembered context, not assert a new journey.
- The exact duration, capacity, and failure conditions of the fictional Ravash remedy and Dust remain open. The draft establishes follow-up care and access needs, not a real medical claim or universal cure.
- Dyia suspects the circle financing medicines and experiments by the end of Nine. He has not identified Hanno as maker. His appointment with a potential witness is written; what he learns before the final door remains unwritten.
- Profiles and world summaries should be reconciled with these draft additions, especially Alethea's institutional role, Amara's recovery work, Hanno's cultivation and limited information, and Dyia's evidence gaps. These changes deepen current continuity without determining the epic's broader primary story.
