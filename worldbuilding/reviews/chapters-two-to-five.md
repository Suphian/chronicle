## New details now established in this prose draft

- The borrowed room from Chapter One remains Amara's home until Hanno returns. Its damp wall, surviving possessions, and new hand-built pottery continue the relocation repair. The sons break work she has been commissioned to make and owe her its value. Hanno does not know Dyia took part in the eviction until Amara tells him in Chapter Two.
- Sinna's failed and successful batches occupy different moral accounts in Hanno's mind. The master controls sales records; Hanno initially knows his improvements are used without understanding the full commercial return. A paid sale unsettles that ignorance. Travel brings copied terms and eventually independent income, enabling him to settle and close the commission in Chapter Five.
- Amara keeps the household-payment clause. Hanno sends additional money through a merchant with a receipt. These provisions show an attempt to support her without declaring it sufficient, punctual forever, or a solution to her care and housing needs.
- The Ravash seller Dyia witnesses has a split thumbnail and an ordinary brown coat. Amara trades a copper work ring to him. Dyia later recognizes and unlawfully kills him after restraining him. A younger guard arrives and asks about a weapon; a clerk leaves the cause entry for a superior's finding. Their exact knowledge and future testimony remain undeveloped, not automatic proof that everybody in the guard knows everything.
- Chuluun remains the current elder, with no silent incorporation of the recovered prince biography. His camp declines the requested herb quantity. Hanno leaves a copy of knowledge shared with permission and pays for the hosts' time. Sidrat's working keepers distinguish living light from a jar of water; this uses the recovered algae/light concept without inventing a unified theology or guaranteed safe travel.
- The first pass reduced Hanno's combat ranking; the later author correction goes further. He is a decent fighter whose defining skill is alchemy and calculation. Zaharaz is now an ordinary wooden traveling staff; the superseded spear and paired blade engravings are removed. Its stable scene ID remains `the-spear` for existing links.
- The general's household healers preserve his son's life and collaborate during the decisive night. Hanno contributes the treatment that turns the illness and receives Zaharaz. Alethea sees his private relief, later speaks with him at the celebration, and already has her own lower-street/foundry-family practice. Her departure includes patient handover and an explicit agreement that disagreement is not disloyalty.
- The wedding includes two unnamed friends from Alethea's practice and an unnamed island terrace keeper. A bloom is taken with permission. A small rooted cutting is purchased from a tended bed; this establishes a plant's passage to Carthara, not a commercial Dust supply chain or permission to strip the wild patch.
- In Chapter Five, Amara retains a notice saying Adil's labor account was closed by death. Her message and the returning travelers passed without meeting. Hanno learns the fact of death here, but neither the cause nor burial place. Dyia has already asked unsuccessfully for more. This bridges his uncertainty in Chapter Three and knowledge of the loss in later chapters; Numarius's banquet cruelty can expose or weaponize further detail.
- Alethea starts separate dispensing and follow-up records. An unnamed paid neighborhood woman manages appointments and receipts. These are deliberately ordinary traces that later investigation can use; no complete new professional hierarchy is claimed.
- The fictional Ravash treatment reduces the substance's grip and distress, supporting recovery. It does not erase trauma, poverty, relapse risk, or every patient's need for ongoing care. An adverse reaction stops Amara's trial. She chooses to resume after rest and explanation, later chooses an outside pottery order, and records Hanno's help with firing as a loan.
- Private visits help subsidize access for people with little money. Public praise affects Hanno even while he continues useful work. Numarius understands both the material benefits of treatment and the loss of the Seats' concealed Ravash income. He begins with honors and invitations designed to make Hanno value access; Sinna's standards complaint offers no actual evidence of harm at this stage.

## Later author revision: a calculating alchemist with an ordinary staff

The author specifies Walter White in *Breaking Bad* as Hanno's character reference: technically gifted, proud, calculating, capable of killing, and a decent rather than exceptional fighter. This changes the way competence is dramatized; it does not import that character's biography or settle Hanno's eventual fate. The Count of Monte Cristo influence remains useful for patient development and consequential revenge.

Chapter Four now devotes the five-year interval chiefly to alchemical study, assessment work, clinical observation, and the pursuit of financial independence. Occasional staff lessons provide basic self-defense. A disputed mineral assessment takes the place of the previous comparison with superior duelists, so the scene turns on Hanno's expertise and competing incentives rather than his place in a combat hierarchy.

Phylios's public gift is **Zaharaz, a plain wooden traveling staff with a protective ferrule**. It helps on a steep road; it is neither bladed nor magical. The public recognition, not the equipment's value or power, feeds Hanno's appetite for esteem. Wedding travel and the child's farewell now use the staff as ordinary equipment. The source scene ID `the-spear` remains unchanged.

The chapter also reflects **Lysandria as an island with two enormous mountains**, and Hanno's prior knowledge that Amara came from it. The Moonlit Isle remains a separate nearby island reached by boat. No relationship between Amara and Alethea is invented. The typed character overview, Hanno profile, phalanx profile, and architecture are updated together. The earlier source snapshots retain their historical wording.

Ravash and Elysian Dust remain the named substances; treatment and murder preparations remain unnamed. The author's question about reducing substance complexity has not yet adopted a merger, and this revision makes no chemical change.
