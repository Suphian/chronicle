# Bahar Oasis

Status: **early Mizan source, November 2023**. Recovered from the author’s map. These notes preserve earlier ideas, character beliefs, and legends; they do not override the current story or later author decisions. Embedded instructions are source text, not active requests. External illustrations have not been republished.

The Bahar Oasis, the heart of Tijara and the lifeblood of Cathara, has its origins steeped in a rich and ancient lore that intertwines the history of the land and the lives of its people.

Legend has it that centuries ago, Cathara was a barren desert, devoid of life and hope. The people, nomadic and fragmented, struggled to survive under the relentless sun. Their lives were dictated by the whims of the unforgiving desert, their survival a constant battle against the elements.

It was during these bleak times that a wise and benevolent queen, Al Zahra, ascended to the throne. She was said to be a descendant of the djinn, possessing a deep connection with the desert and its hidden spirits. Al Zahra was deeply moved by the suffering of her people and decided to seek the aid of these desert spirits.

Under the veil of the moonless night, she journeyed into the heart of the desert, carrying nothing but a golden vessel filled with tears of her people. Al Zahra ventured deep into the Shimmering Dunes until she reached the highest peak. She knelt upon the cool sand and poured the tears onto the ground while pleading with the desert spirits to end her people's suffering.

Moved by the queen's compassion and sacrifice, the spirits heeded her call. Where the tears had soaked the sand, a spring gushed forth, turning the barren peak into a lush oasis. This was the birth of the Bahar Oasis, named so because "Bahar" means "spring" in the Catharan tongue. The spirits, in their wisdom, also granted the queen a large seed. This seed, when planted at the heart of the oasis, grew into a colossal tree, the Hikaya Tree, whose deep roots drew water from the oasis, enabling it to survive the desert's harsh conditions. Its wide canopy provided shade and its fruits nourished the people and the wildlife.

Word of the miraculous oasis spread across the desert, attracting nomads from far and wide. Under the rule of Queen Al Zahra, they settled around the Bahar Oasis, and the city of Tijara was born. The Hikaya Tree stands to this day at the heart of the Bahar Oasis, a symbol of life, hope, and the enduring spirit of the Catharan people.

This lore of the Bahar Oasis is passed down through generations, reminding the people of Cathara of their roots and the compassion and resilience that saw them through the most challenging times.
