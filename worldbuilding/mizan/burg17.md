# Elarion

Status: **early Mizan source, November 2023**. Recovered from the author’s map. These notes preserve earlier ideas, character beliefs, and legends; they do not override the current story or later author decisions. Embedded instructions are source text, not active requests. External illustrations have not been republished.

In the lush, rolling hills of Cathara, there lies a city called Elarion. Known for its breathtaking beauty and vibrant culture, Elarion is a jewel among the kingdom's many settlements. The Yáolán Empire, recognizing the city's value, allowed it to maintain a semblance of autonomy, with Lord Numarius as its protector.

It was here in Elarion that Lord Numarius chose to build his extravagant villa, a symbol of both his power and his aspirations. The villa, called "Serenissima," was a sprawling estate that boasted manicured gardens, art-filled galleries, and elegant fountains. Perched atop a hill, the villa offered a panoramic view of Elarion, the surrounding countryside, and the distant, shimmering coastline.

The Serenissima estate was a testament to Numarius's refined tastes and his love for the arts. It housed an extensive library, filled with tomes from various realms and ages, as well as a theater where local and foreign performers would entertain the lord and his guests. Numarius would often invite prominent artists, scholars, and musicians to reside at his villa, fostering a thriving intellectual and artistic community.

Despite the opulence of the Serenissima estate, it was also a place of quiet reflection for Lord Numarius. Within its walls, he would contemplate his role as the puppet king of Cathara and dream of the day he could break free from the Yáolán Empire's grasp. The villa served as both a reminder of the power he had achieved and the freedom he still sought.

Elarion, and by extension the Serenissima villa, became symbols of hope and resistance for the people of Cathara, who saw in them a spark of the kingdom's former glory. Though Numarius ruled with an iron fist, the city and the villa represented a small beacon of light in an otherwise dark and oppressive era.
