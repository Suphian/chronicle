# The Khanate of the Leonin Tribes - The Fragmented Khanate

Status: **early Mizan source, November 2023**. Recovered from the author’s map. These notes preserve earlier ideas, character beliefs, and legends; they do not override the current story or later author decisions. Embedded instructions are source text, not active requests. External illustrations have not been republished.

Once a unified political entity, the Khanate of the Leonin Tribes consisted of fifteen independent tribes, each contributing to the strength and resilience of the collective Leonin society. Each tribe occupied a distinct region of the expansive and harsh Tengeri Wastes, adapting and evolving its practices to thrive in the specific conditions of its territory.

These tribes, including notable ones like the Altan and Tsetseg, operated autonomously, honoring their unique customs, traditions, and self-governance. However, all were bound by the authority of the Khan, a figurehead who ensured unity, arbitrated intertribal disputes, and led the collective defense against external threats.

However, for many generations now, no Khan has presided over the tribes. The Khanate has descended into dormancy, its unity fractured. A series of unfortunate events, including natural disasters, internal conflict, and foreign invasions, contributed to the fall of the Khanate, but one event is recalled with particular pain - the loss of the fifteenth tribe.

The fifteenth tribe, whose name is spoken in hushed whispers, disappeared mysteriously, leaving behind only abandoned dwellings and unanswered questions. Their loss shook the Leonin society, driving the final wedge in the already crumbling unity of the Khanate.

Today, the remaining fourteen tribes continue their existence in the Tengeri Wastes, holding onto their customs and traditions, but the unity and strength once offered by the Khanate are distant memories. Intertribal disputes have become more frequent, and the collective defense is weakened in the absence of a unifying Khan.

The Golden Yurt, once the symbol of the Khan's authority and the Khanate's unity, stands empty in the heart of Erdene Zuu. The seat of a power that once was, it now serves as a poignant reminder of the unity that once prevailed among the Leonin tribes. It stands as a silent testament to the strength and resilience of the Leonin people, and a somber monument to their lost brethren.

Without the Khanate, the tribes have become more insular, focusing on their survival in the unforgiving Tengeri Wastes. Though they share common blood and history, the absence of the fifteenth tribe and the vacant position of the Khan have created visible rifts among them. Disputes over resources, territorial boundaries, and old rivalries have intensified, threatening the delicate balance that keeps the tribes from spiraling into chaos.

Yet, even in these trying times, there's a shared longing among the tribes for the return of the Khanate. Many Leonin elders recount tales of the once flourishing Khanate to their young ones, embedding in them the hope for a leader who can reunite the tribes and restore their former glory. The tribes continue to uphold their traditions, train their warriors, and hold tribal councils, keeping the spirit of the Khanate alive.

Amidst this, the dormant Khanate awaits the rise of a new Khan, a leader who can mend the fractured bonds and reunite the tribes. The lost tribe continues to be a wound in the heart of the Leonin people, a mystery they yearn to solve, and a reminder of a unity they desire to reclaim.

In the Tengeri Wastes, the fifteen Leonin tribes of the Khanate face the harsh reality of their homeland being caught in the crossfire of a regional military conflict between two global superpowers. Each tribe is affected in its own way, with the impact determined by their proximity to the conflict and how it directly disrupts their way of life. Despite the fractured state of the Khanate, the tribes maintain a sense of solidarity, understanding that their survival depends on their connections to one another.

Six of the fifteen tribes are cadet branches of the same royal dynasty, the Arslan Dynasty, with their leaders being siblings. The Altan Tribe, from which Chuluun Altan hails, is led by his father, Chief Commander Batbayar Arslan. Chuluun's five uncles rule the remaining five Arslan Dynasty tribes:

Chuluun's father, the Chief Commander of the Altan Tribe, is named Batbayar Arslan. The name Batbayar is Mongolian in origin, with "bat" meaning strong, firm, or stable, and "bayar" meaning joy or happiness. Together, Batbayar can be translated as "strong happiness" or "lasting joy." This name suggests that Chuluun's father is a source of strength and happiness for his family and his tribe, and that his leadership is both enduring and uplifting.

Uncle Tömörbaatar Arslan - Leader of the Tumen Tribe

Tömörbaatar Arslan - "Iron Hero": Tömörbaatar is derived from the Mongolian word "tömör," meaning iron, and "baatar," meaning hero. This name suggests strength and resilience

Tumen Tribe - "Ten Thousand": Tumen is a Mongolian word meaning ten thousand, symbolizing the tribe's strength in numbers and their unity.

Uncle Nergüi Arslan - Leader of the Süld Tribe

Nergüi Arslan - "No Name": Nergüi is a Mongolian name that literally means "no name." This name is often given to protect the child from evil spirits, as it is believed that spirits cannot harm someone with no name.

Süld Tribe - "Flame" or "Spirit": Süld is a Mongolian word that means flame or spirit, representing the tribe's fiery determination and strong spirit.

Uncle Zorig Arslan - Leader of the Khar-Nuur Tribe

Zorig Arslan - "Destiny": Zorig is a Mongolian name that means destiny or fate, indicating that this uncle may have a strong sense of purpose and the potential to shape the future.

Khar-Nuur Tribe - "Black Lake": Khar-Nuur is derived from the Mongolian words "khar," meaning black, and "nuur," meaning lake. This name may suggest that the tribe's territory features a prominent dark lake or that they possess a sense of mystery or depth.

Uncle Jargal Arslan - Leader of the Tölöö Tribe

Jargal Arslan - "Happiness": Jargal is a Mongolian name that means happiness, suggesting that this uncle is a source of joy and positivity within the family and tribe.

Tölöö Tribe - "Pinnacle": Tölöö is a Mongolian word that means pinnacle, indicating that this tribe may be situated in a high, mountainous region, or symbolically representing their aspiration to reach the peak of their potential.

Uncle Erdene Arslan - Leader of the Gazar Tribe

Erdene Arslan - "Jewel" or "Precious": Erdene is a Mongolian name that means jewel or precious, implying that this uncle holds significant value and importance within the family and the tribe.

Gazar Tribe - "Earth": Gazar is a Mongolian word meaning earth, symbolizing the tribe's strong connection to the land and nature, as well as their grounded, practical nature.

These six tribes, bound by blood and loyalty, are centered around the capital, Erdene Zuu, located in the heart of the Tengeri Wastes. They form a bulwark of stability, striving to maintain a semblance of order and unity amidst the chaos of the ongoing conflict.

As the military tensions escalate, the tribes of the Arslan Dynasty, along with the other tribes in the Khanate, grapple with the challenges brought on by the conflict. They must navigate their relationships with the two warring superpowers, balance the need for self-preservation against the call for solidarity, and protect their people from the devastation of war.

In these turbulent times, the actions of Chuluun Altan and the choices he makes on his personal journey will have far-reaching consequences. His destiny is not only intertwined with that of his own tribe, the Altan, but also with the fate of the entire Khanate, whose future hangs in the balance as they face the unrelenting forces of conflict and upheaval.

The lost tribe, remembered by the other Leonin tribes, was once located in the far north across the great plains, ruling over a major mountain peak. The tribe belonged to the Mergen Dynasty and was led by five royal brothers, known for their striking all-white appearance. The lost tribe is called the Tsast-Urmai Tribe, symbolizing a mysterious and elusive quality, with "tsast" meaning "mirage" and "urmai" meaning "echo" in Mongolian.

The five brothers who ruled the Tsast-Urmai Tribe were:

Batmunkh Mergen - Batmunkh means "strong and eternal" in Mongolian, reflecting his steadfastness and enduring spirit.

Tserendorj Mergen - Tserendorj means "smith of wisdom," suggesting a wise and knowledgeable leader.

Erdenechimeg Mergen - Erdenechimeg means "jewel of wisdom," symbolizing his deep understanding and intellectual prowess.

Bilegt Mergen - Bilegt means "stronghold of fortune," representing his ability to bring prosperity and good fortune to his tribe.

Adiyabold Mergen - Adiyabold means "golden power," signifying his strength, courage, and commanding presence.

Before the Mergen Dynasty and the Tsast-Urmai Tribe were swallowed by the war between the two neighboring superpowers, they were a respected and powerful presence among the Leonin tribes. Their mysterious disappearance has left a lasting impact on the collective memory of the remaining tribes, who still refer to them as the lost tribe.

While the majority of the Leonin grieve for the loss of the Tsast-Urmai Tribe, believing them to have been exterminated, there are a select few among the Arslan Dynasty who secretly feel a sense of relief and even joy at the Mergen Dynasty's demise. The roots of this sentiment can be traced back to the fact that the ancestors of the Arslan and Mergen Dynasties were once brothers. This familial connection led some circles within the Mergen Dynasty to lay claims to the Khanate throne, creating a rivalry between the two dynasties.

The destruction of the Mergen Dynasty, in the eyes of some members of the Arslan Dynasty, has eliminated a potential threat to their own claims to leadership and influence among the Leonin tribes. However, this sentiment is not shared by all within the Arslan Dynasty, and the knowledge of this secret joy is carefully guarded, as it could potentially create tension and distrust among the remaining tribes.

The complex relationship between the Arslan and Mergen Dynasties adds another layer of intrigue and political maneuvering to the already intricate tapestry of the Leonin tribes and their ongoing struggles against external threats and internal strife.

The two tribes in the most southern part of the Tengeri Wastes, southwest of the capital Erdene Zuu, belong to the Ulzii Dynasty. These tribes enjoy a more prosperous and peaceful existence compared to their northern brethren, thanks to their strategic location between two peaceful nations and their control over an entire coastline rich in resources, food, and trade opportunities. The two tribes are led by brothers:

Brother Odval Ulzii - Leader of the Tavan-Tenger Tribe

Odval is a Mongolian name that means "star." As a leader, Odval may be seen as a guiding light for his people, directing them towards prosperity and safety. The Tavan-Tenger Tribe's name is derived from the Mongolian words "tavan," meaning five, and "tenger," meaning sky or heavens. The name symbolizes a connection with the spiritual realm and suggests that the tribe enjoys divine favor and protection.

Brother Ochir Ulzii - Leader of the Jinst-Uul Tribe

Ochir is a Mongolian name that means "strong" or "powerful." As a leader, Ochir embodies strength and resilience, ensuring the continued prosperity and stability of his tribe. The Jinst-Uul Tribe's name is derived from the Mongolian words "jinst," meaning gold, and "uul," meaning mountain. This name implies that the tribe's territory contains valuable resources, such as gold mines, which contribute to their wealth and affluence.

The Ulzii Dynasty's tribes are more focused on maintaining their prosperous way of life and nurturing their relationships with their peaceful neighbors than engaging in the ongoing conflict. While they are somewhat detached from the struggles of the northern tribes, their prosperity and stability provide a contrasting perspective on life in the Tengeri Wastes.

The tribe from the Khulan Dynasty, known for their strong and imposing figures with wider heads, is led by a brave and fierce warrior named Barsbold Khulan. Their tribe, the Tsagaan-Amgalan Tribe, is situated just north of the capital, Erdene Zuu, and is currently the only tribe completely surrounded by one of the warring superpowers. Despite the military occupation in their lands, the Tsagaan-Amgalan Tribe remains a bastion of fierce resistance, refusing to yield to the invaders.

Barsbold is a Mongolian name that means "firm hero" or "strong warrior," reflecting his unyielding resolve and courage in the face of adversity. The Tsagaan-Amgalan Tribe's name is derived from the Mongolian words "tsagaan," meaning white, and "amgalan," meaning peace or tranquility. The name symbolizes their desire for peace and freedom in their homeland, as well as their resilience and determination to protect their way of life against the encroaching superpower.

They are ruled by a Leonin leader who appears far more human than lion, named Arban Naran. His name reflects his more human-like appearance, with "Arban" meaning "ten" in Mongolian and "Naran" meaning "sun," symbolizing a unique and radiant presence.

Arban Naran leads the Uulyn-KhökhTribe, . They are strategically fortified atop a high mountain peak, neighboring an extremely robust trading center known as the Majlis Gate. This area is rich with tunnel networks that traverse the mountainous region, providing the tribe with valuable trading opportunities and a strong defensive position.

Tribe: Uulyn-Khökh Tribe - "Mountain's Crown" (derived from the Mongolian words "uul," meaning mountain, and "khökh," meaning crown or top, signifying the tribe's location at the peak of a high mountain)

While the Uulyn-Khökh Tribe may not be as directly involved in the conflict as some of the other tribes, they use their intelligence and ability to blend in with the crowd to maintain a noble, low-intensity resistance movement. They focus on more subversive operations, leveraging their strategic position and connections to the Majis Gate to subtly undermine their enemies and support their fellow Leonin tribes in the struggle.
