# Qal'a Ruins

Status: **early Mizan source, November 2023**. Recovered from the author’s map. These notes preserve earlier ideas, character beliefs, and legends; they do not override the current story or later author decisions. Embedded instructions are source text, not active requests. External illustrations have not been republished.

According to the legends and lore, there was once a vibrant city known as Al Qal'a, which in the ancient Catharan language means "The Fortress". This capital city was of the kingdom of Safara, grander than the present-day Tijara. Safara was ruled by the wise and just King Malik Al Asad and his Queen, Al Zahra, who held an esteemed place in the Hadlakawn Triumvirate alongside the kingdoms of Anket and Majis.

Al Qal'a, famous for its grandeur and impenetrable fortifications made of a mysterious substance called Suraj Stones, was a beacon of prosperity and culture. Scholars, traders, and artisans gathered in droves, drawn to the city's unparalleled advancements in astronomy, architecture, and irrigation systems.

However, jealousy and greed sparked conflict with neighboring kingdoms, Anket and Majlis kingdoms instigated a surprise attack on Al Qal'a. A massive army from the Anket Kingdom, was bribed by the Majlis Gate kingdom to carry out the attack.

Despite the formidable defenses of Al Qal'a, King Malik Al Asad knew they were vastly outnumbered. In desperation, he sought the help of his ancestors, who were believed to hold mystical powers to change the course of the battle. The ancestors agreed to help but demanded the abandonment of Al Qal’a.

The King & Queen invoked the ancestors, who responded by stirring a tremendous windstorm that swept across the desert, picking up fine sand and dust. This windstorm, the first Sirocco, forced the invaders to retreat. The spirits decided to leave the Sirocco as a perpetual storm, creating the Veil Sirocco, a formidable natural barrier shielding Cathara from potential aggressors.

The Veil of Sirocco is an enigmatic natural phenomenon that has inspired tales of awe and mystery in Catharan culture. Created as a result of a sacred ritual performed by Queen Al Zahra, it served as a defense against marauding Kingdoms.

During the successful repelling the invaders, King Malik Al Asad, honoring the agreement with the ancestors, led his people to abandon Al Qal'a. They migrated to the Bahar Oasis, the site of the present city of Tijara. The once-great city of Al Qal'a was left to the sands of time, eventually becoming the Qal'a Ruins, a testament to the pact with the ancestors and a poignant reminder of the sacrifices made.

Today, the Qal'a Ruins stand as a testament to a bygone era, a symbol of sacrifice, and a beacon for archaeologists and adventurers.
