## Continuity defects and unresolved mechanics

**Age and elapsed time — unresolved, high priority.** Hanno is 115 in the profile and questionnaire. The same Numarius, Sinna, and market witnesses span his childhood and adulthood; the vendors are only now described as old. No lifespan or age for those people is established. Half-elven ancestry alone does not date his apprenticeship or explain the rest of the cast. Record Hanno's age at the drought, Dyia's guard entry, each journey, marriage, cure, banquet, and knock; then calculate everyone's corresponding ages. Choose a shorter chronology, explicitly long-lived supporting cast, or succession only after assessing consequences. Do not silently turn Numarius into multiple people.

**Childhood compression — missing transitions.** Chapter One moves from a young boy watching the stall to the younger brother taking a guard post before the father's formal sentencing. Plausible elapsed years, child recruitment, or an extended detention might explain this, but none is stated. Chapter Two mentions years of unspoken pain. A dated chronology should determine which explanation actually fits.

**The confiscated home — apparent scene continuity gap.** Chapter One removes the rooms and pottery wheel; Chapter Two returns to the family's dwelling with pottery and herbs, without saying where Amara now lives. Establish a relocation or the terms of continued occupation. Amara's housing during Hanno's years away also needs tracking.

**Iskandar's army — arithmetic versus legend.** Fifty thousand arrive; he loses many over three years; fifty thousand leave and not one stays. Read literally, these numbers conflict unless replenishment occurred. Keep the number as an explicitly repeated feature of the song, or revise the count once the legend's reliability is chosen. No replenishment is currently established.

**Hanno's knowledge of Sinna — missing evidence.** Readers see the murder, but Hanno's route from Amara's death and Sinna's accusations to certainty about the killer is not shown. Decide what he learns and whether his certainty is justified. Likewise, a shipment killing its thief does not by itself prove the cure safe or clear Hanno of earlier accusations.

**Numarius's change in risk tolerance — missing motivation.** He recognizes Hanno's ruthlessness after Sinna's death, then deliberately humiliates him. This can be credible political intimidation, but the protection, miscalculation, or immediate objective behind it needs a scene. His ignorance of resentment should not erase his established knowledge of danger.

**Drug trade — over-absolute narration.** Unknown cargo does not create an untraceable distribution system. Someone must knowingly unpack, price, sell, and collect payment for Dust. Hanno also needs a specific channel for learning Dyia's investigative steps. Replace omniscient claims such as “No thread leads back to him” with an observable arrangement that can fail. Track cultivation, harvest season, production capacity, port inspection, storage, money, and who knows each link; leave operational chemical details outside the story bible.

**Healing — define limits before adding miracles.** The Ravash cure is treated as ending the epidemic and driving citywide prosperity. Specify whether it treats acute effects, dependence, relapse risk, or all of these through fantasy medicine, plus cost, capacity, and access. Do not equate treatment with erasing trauma, poverty, or coercion. These are fictional rules to choose, not medical claims about real addiction.

**Faith — an incomplete institution.** Aetherium Harmonia currently consists mostly of Hanno's equilibrium vocabulary and questionnaire tenets. His justification for poisoning people should not automatically become its authentic teaching. Develop credible believers who disagree with his reading, along with practices and institutions, before assigning the whole culture a moral position.

**Geography source — claims need levels of certainty.** Distinguish listed places and institutions from in-world beliefs about curses, healing water, protective spirits, or divine origins. The recovered description promises completely safe travel and abundant provision in Sidrat while also naming a treacherous pass, a hostile western neighbor, and a defended fortress. This is an unresolved tension, not permission to erase either description. Define the extent, cost, and exceptions of sanctuary. Leonin subsistence, interregional trade, and Ruhania's watershed likewise need material rules before map distances or chapter travel times are fixed.

## Character depth work

### Alethea needs a plot of her own

She is described as exceptionally wise, yet primarily heals Hanno emotionally, supports his research, waits for him, and dies to make him understand himself. The clinic is a promising independent setting, but it arrives late. “Raised in Lysandria” does not adequately explain silence or passivity, and addiction should not be reduced to insufficient wisdom.

Before expanding prose, give her an objective that would still matter without Hanno; colleagues or patients with their own claims on her; a material dependency; and at least one consequential decision he cannot control. Let her clinical knowledge produce questions, evidence, and action. Establish what she recognizes about the flower, what she suspects, and what she can prove. If Chapter Ten remains, make the consequences of her work survive her. Her death is a draft choice to evaluate, not the only way to reveal Hanno's guilt. Changing it should be an explicit revision proposal.

### Hanno needs limits beyond self-deception

The sources make him a brilliant alchemist, exceptional archer, master swordsman, best regional spearman after five years, charismatic merchant, superior judge of people, and nearly perfect criminal organizer. Those claims make everyone around him less necessary. His emotional blindness is compelling but cannot carry every limitation.

Recommended revision, not established fact: keep alchemy his strongest craft; make other proficiencies specific, maintained at a cost, and dependent on partners. Define what Zaharaz does **not** solve. Let local specialists exceed him in their own field. Give successful cures collaborators and failed cases; give his business obligations he cannot outwit. The author's knowledge of a character's blind spot should not make that character foolish whenever a plot needs concealment.

### Dyia and the Seats need constraints, not just motives

Dyia's extrajudicial killings already complicate his protective mission. Track witnesses, records, discipline, loyalty, and victims' families. His final choice should endanger something tangible beyond his feelings about Hanno.

Numarius, Corvo, and Vael hold useful sources of power: food and debt, harbor and trade, armed force. Give each a constituency that can withdraw support, an expense they must meet, a rule they sometimes obey, and someone whose cooperation they need. Otherwise the triumvirate becomes three interchangeable villains. The current map's single-ruler summary of Carthara underrepresents the newer three-seat arrangement.

## World depth before world size

Sophistication comes from obligations colliding, not a larger list of names. Establish five connected systems first: water and food; land and debt; shipping and seasonal travel; medicine and access; law and enforcement. For each, record who works, who pays, who authorizes, who benefits, and what happens when it fails. The story should include residents with ordinary projects outside Hanno's revenge.

The requested futuristic Mediterranean and Middle Eastern architecture can express those systems: decide who funds and maintains a monumental public space, who receives its water or cooling, and who is kept outside. Islamic historical inspiration should include scholarship, worship, trade, care, family, and disagreement as well as warfare. Historical research and proposed adaptations belong in separate notes; the user's remembered pilgrim-ship attack is not yet a canonical incident in this world.

Avoid describing an entire people as a single character trait. The current Bakhtari “no cities” and perpetual-war claims, Leonin nature reverence, and universal Lysandrian balance are useful starting motifs but need internal variation and a stated narrator perspective.
