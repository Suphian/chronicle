# The Lysandrian Phalanx

ID: `the-phalanx` · Existing draft warrior society

## Established in current draft

Lysandrian spear fighters who prize balance. Their island has two enormous mountains. Hanno lives and studies there for five years, chiefly working in alchemy and care; occasional lessons give him practical self-defense with a staff. He is a decent fighter, not a phalanx champion, military commander, or unusually gifted combatant. Phylios presents **Zaharaz, an ordinary wooden traveling staff**, after the collaborative work that saves his son. The gift honors useful knowledge and aid, not martial accomplishment. The optional highland legend recalls an earlier unsuccessful Lysandrian invasion of Bakhtar.

Sources: [`codex.ts`](../../src/content/codex.ts); Chapters [Zero](../../src/content/chapters/00-the-conqueror.ts) and [Four](../../src/content/chapters/04-gift-of-lysandria.ts).

## Interpretation

A military formation's discipline, supply, and authority are different from a traveler's ability to protect himself. The phalanx can remain a capable institution without lending Hanno its prestige as a fighter. He may learn from its practitioners while depending on other people for protection and armed force.

## Proposed development

Establish recruitment, length of service, island supply, and the relationship between military workshops and civilian practitioners. Give a soldier a responsibility that does not involve training Hanno. Decide whether the claimed failure in Bakhtar is taught honestly, commemorated defensively, or contested. A doctrine of balance can justify restraint to one officer and militarized hierarchy to another.

## Open questions

Is this an army, elite order, civic duty, or school? Who may leave? Who pays for metals, food, and transport between harbor and mountain settlements? The author's latest correction supersedes both the old “best spear in the region” claim and the first pass's dueling comparisons: Hanno's distinguishing ability is alchemical calculation, not combat. The early Sparta comparison is an influence, not a completed account of institutions.
